Nanomedicine is a manufacturing problem first納米醫學，首先是一個生產問題

A beautiful particle in the lab is the easy part. Making the same particle a hundred times is where most projects stall.在實驗室做出一顆漂亮的粒子是容易的部分。把同一顆粒子做一百次，才是大部分項目卡住的地方。

Biotech生物科技4 Nov 20262026年11月4日4 min read閱讀約 4 分鐘

Most conversations about nanomedicine start with the exciting part: a particle that finds a tumour, carries a drug past the body's defences, and releases it in the right place. I understand why. It is genuinely exciting.

But I have come to believe the harder problem sits somewhere much less glamorous. Can you make that particle the same way, every time?

One good batch proves very little

A single batch with the right size and the right loading is a result. Ten batches with the same size distribution, the same purity and the same stability is a product. The gap between those two is where a lot of nano projects quietly stall.

Small changes matter at this scale. A slightly different mixing speed, a few degrees of temperature, an impurity that was never measured — any of these can shift particle size, and size changes where the particle ends up in the body. A formulation that worked beautifully on the bench can behave differently at ten times the volume.

Regulators care about this too

In drug development this falls under what regulators call CMC — chemistry, manufacturing and controls. It is not the part that makes headlines, but it is the part that has to hold up when someone asks how you know batch 50 is the same as batch 1.

Why we started with process

This is why the patent work around my company has a deliberately unglamorous side. Alongside the granted formulation patents, the equipment filings on my list cover preparing drug solutions, separating by centrifugation, purifying nanomaterials and sieving material for tablets. None of them is a cure for anything. They are the plumbing that makes a cure manufacturable.

I would rather build that foundation early than discover the problem after the exciting data.

A question I think investors should ask more

Before the efficacy slide, ask for the batch-to-batch data. How many batches, how consistent, at what scale? The answer tells you a lot about how far a nanomedicine project really is from patients.

談到納米醫學，大部分討論都從最令人興奮的部分開始：一顆能找到腫瘤的粒子，帶著藥物避過身體的防禦，在正確的位置釋放。我明白為甚麼，因為這真的令人興奮。

但我愈來愈相信，更難的問題在一個不起眼得多的地方：你能否每一次都把那顆粒子做得一模一樣？

一批做得好，其實證明不了太多

一批尺寸正確、載藥量正確的粒子，是一個結果。十批尺寸分佈、純度和穩定性都一致的粒子，才是一個產品。兩者之間的距離，正是很多納米項目悄悄停下來的地方。

在這個尺度，細微的改變都很重要。混合速度稍有不同、溫度差幾度、一種從未量度過的雜質，都可能改變粒子大小；而粒子大小，決定它最終去到身體哪裏。在實驗台上表現完美的配方，放大到十倍體積後，表現可以完全不同。

監管機構同樣重視這一點

在藥物研發中，這屬於監管機構所說的 CMC，即化學、生產及控制。它不是上頭條的部分，但當有人問「你怎樣知道第 50 批和第 1 批一樣」時，必須站得住腳。

為甚麼我們從工藝開始

所以我公司的專利工作，刻意有一個不起眼的部分。除了已授權的配方專利，我名下的設備申請涵蓋藥液配製、離心分離、納米材料純化及片劑篩分。它們沒有一項能治好任何病，但它們是令療法可以被生產出來的「管道」。

我寧願早一點打好這個基礎，也不想在拿到令人興奮的數據之後，才發現生產出了問題。

我認為投資者應該多問的一個問題

在看療效那一頁之前，先要批次之間的數據：做了多少批？一致性如何？在甚麼規模？答案會告訴你，這個納米醫學項目距離病人其實還有多遠。

— 鄭迪珉 Tyler Tik Man Cheng
