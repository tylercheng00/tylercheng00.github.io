Founders often open a first meeting with the valuation they have in mind. I understand the impulse. It is also the wrong opening. The question sitting on the other side of the table is usually simpler, and harder: who owns this company, and who is allowed to bind it?

Until that is plain, a valuation is a number attached to an object the investor cannot yet describe.

### Draw the box before you price it

A US investor looking at a China-based business is trying to see three things at once. Where the operations sit. Where the contracts sit. Where the shares they would buy sit. If those are three different places, say so on one page, with arrows. Do not wait to be asked in the third meeting. By then the investor has either built a wrong picture, or decided the picture is not worth building.

The page does not need to be elegant. It needs to be true. A messy structure described clearly is safer than a simple structure described vaguely.

### Who can sign

Ownership is not the same as control, and control is not the same as the person in the room. If a founder needs a parent's consent, a board that has not met, or a shareholder overseas who does not answer email, that is a fact about the round. It belongs in the first conversation. Discovering it at signing is how closings slip by a month.

I ask a blunt version of this early: if we agreed on terms on Friday, who has to say yes before Monday, and are they already informed? The answer is a timetable.

### The offshore company is not a costume

Putting a holding company in the Cayman Islands, or anywhere else, does not answer the question. It moves the question. Investors will still ask what that holding company owns, through which agreements, and what happens to those agreements if the rules at home change. If the answer is "our lawyer handled it", you do not yet have an answer you can give.

You should be able to explain the chain without the lawyer in the room. The lawyer's job is to make the chain true. Your job is to know it.

### Say the awkward part yourself

Every structure has a feature that is annoying to explain. A nominee. A variable interest arrangement. A relative on the register. An option pool that was promised and never documented. Say it first, in one sentence, and say what you have done about it. Investors are rarely shocked by a structure. They are shocked when the second version of the story does not match the first.

Valuation can wait until both sides are talking about the same company.

創辦人第一次見面，常常先說自己心目中的估值。我明白這個衝動。這也是錯誤的開場。桌子對面的人，心裏的問題通常更簡單，也更難：這家公司是誰的，誰有權拘束它？

這件事還沒說清楚之前，估值只是一個數字，貼在一件投資者還描述不了的東西上。

### 先畫出盒子，再定價

看一家中國公司的美國投資者，同時在看三件事。業務在哪裏。合約在哪裏。他們要買的股份在哪裏。如果是三個不同的地方，用一頁紙畫出來，加上箭嘴。不要等到第三次會才被問。到那時，對方要麼已經建立了一個錯誤的圖像，要麼決定這個圖像不值得建。

這一頁不必漂亮。它必須是真的。一個複雜的架構說得清楚，好過一個簡單的架構說得含糊。

### 誰可以簽字

擁有權不是控制權，控制權也不是坐在房間裏的那個人。如果創辦人需要父母同意、一個還沒開過的董事會，或者一位不回郵件的海外股東，這就是這一輪融資的事實。它屬於第一次對話。到了簽字才發現，成交就會滑走一個月。

我會很早問一個直接的版本：如果星期五談妥條款，星期一之前誰必須說好，而他們是否已經知道？答案就是一張時間表。

### 境外公司不是一件外衣

在開曼或其他地方放一家控股公司，並沒有回答問題。它只是把問題移走。投資者仍會問：這家控股公司擁有甚麼、透過哪些協議、如果境內規則改變，那些協議會怎樣。如果答案是「律師處理了」，你還沒有一個自己能說的答案。

你應該可以在沒有律師的房間裏解釋這條鏈。律師的工作是讓這條鏈是真的。你的工作是知道它。

### 彆扭的部分，自己先說

每個架構都有一處解釋起來不舒服。代持。協議控制。名冊上的親戚。答應了卻沒有文件的購股權池。用一句話先說出來，並說明你做了甚麼。投資者很少被架構本身嚇到。他們被嚇到，是第二個版本的故事跟第一個對不上。

等雙方說的是同一家公司，再談估值。
