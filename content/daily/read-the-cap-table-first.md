::: en
When the story and the cap table disagree, I believe the table. The deck is an argument. The table is a claim about who owns what, and at some point a person signs beside it.

A table that leaves out the option pool, or the notes, "because they're small", is a different company. Small things add up at the moment new money comes in. Ask for it fully diluted, and ask for everything that can become a share. Then ask which of those rows people have actually signed. A promise in a chat is not a row. It can still be a problem.

The interesting column is not always the percentage. Someone with eight percent who has to approve any new round, or any sale, is not an eight percent holder in any practical sense. They are a gate. "They've always supported us" is not the same as support you can choose to skip. Name the gates. If there are a lot of them, the new investor can feel that this round is really a negotiation with your own history.

A pool "to be created" at the round is dilution with a friendly name. So is a pool that was granted in conversations and never minuted. Put both on the table before you talk about price. A price on the wrong share count is a misunderstanding you will have again at the lawyers. I would rather argue about it in week one.

A founder, not the lawyer, should be able to walk down every row. Who this is. Why they have it. What they can block. Whether the paper exists. If the founder can't, the company doesn't know its own ownership. The cover of the deck will not fix that.

::: zh
故事和股權表打架的時候，我信表。簡報是一個說法。表是在講誰擁有甚麼，到後面會有人在旁邊簽名。

「因為很小」所以漏掉購股權池、或者那些票據，那是另一家公司。小東西會在新錢進來的時候加起來。要完全攤薄的，並列出所有可以變成股份的東西。然後問：哪些是人家真的簽過的。聊天裡的一句承諾不是一行。它仍然可以是麻煩。

有趣的那一欄不一定是百分比。百分之八，但新的一輪或出售都要他點頭，實際上不是一個百分之八的股東。他是一扇門。「他們一直支持我們」和「你可以不找他」，不是同一件事。把這些門寫出來。門太多的話，新投資者感覺得到：這一輪其實是在跟你自己的歷史談。

這一輪「即將設立」的池，是一件名字比較好聽的攤薄。聊天裡發過、會議記錄裡沒有的，也是。談價格之前先放上桌。股數錯了的價格，你會在律師那裡再吵一次。我寧願第一週吵。

應該是創辦人，不是律師，可以把每一行講完。這是誰。為甚麼有。可以擋住甚麼。文件在不在。創辦人講不了，公司就不知道自己是誰的。簡報封面補不了。
