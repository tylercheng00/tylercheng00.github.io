By early September 2026, only three China-based companies had completed a US listing this year, by one market count. For anyone who watched small Chinese issuers arrive on Nasdaq in batches over the past few years, that is a striking drop.

The most direct cause is the rule the SEC approved on 14 May 2026. Companies that principally operate in mainland China, Hong Kong or Macau now need a firm-commitment offering that raises at least US$25 million to list on Nasdaq through an IPO. The test looks beyond where a company is incorporated: headquarters and where the business is actually run count too. Many recent Chinese IPOs raised only a few million dollars. That route is now effectively closed.

### The door is narrower, not shut

It is easy to read this as "the US no longer wants Chinese companies". I read it differently. The rule is aimed at *small*, not at *China*. Nasdaq's stated concern is that tiny offerings with thin trading are easier to manipulate.

This year's successful listings fit that reading. One China-based company that listed on Nasdaq in June raised roughly US$51 million. A Shenzhen company that listed on the NYSE in August raised roughly US$94 million. Both were well clear of the new line.

So a US listing has gone from "meet the minimum and give it a try" to "be big enough, and be ready". For smaller companies, I think three directions deserve a serious look.

### 1. Grow first, then list

If the business can only support a raise of a few million dollars today, forcing a listing may not help. Many small-cap Chinese stocks have struggled after listing: volatile prices, little liquidity, a low valuation and a heavy compliance bill. Spending a year or two building revenue, profit and a clear story can be the more valuable choice.

### 2. Compare markets again

Nasdaq is not the only option. The NYSE, NYSE American, and the Hong Kong Main Board and GEM each have different thresholds, investor bases and valuation logic. Biotech and deep-tech companies in particular should ask which market's investors best understand their sector, not which name sounds most impressive.

### 3. Be careful with shortcuts

Higher thresholds always bring "workaround" offers, such as listing on the over-the-counter market first, or buying a shell and uplisting later. The new rules set public-float requirements for uplisting too, and an old shell can carry undisclosed debts or compliance problems. Any shortcut deserves full due diligence first.

### A filter, and a reminder

For the market, this rule is a filter. For founders, it is a reminder: a listing is a financing tool, not the finish line. Planning the raise size, the venue and the compliance path early matters more than chasing any particular window.

2026 年已經走到第十一個月，中國企業赴美上市的數字卻冷清得有點意外。有市場統計指出，由年初到 9 月初，完成美股上市的中概企業只有三間。對比過去幾年中小型企業成批登陸納斯達克的景象，落差非常明顯。

最直接的原因，是今年 5 月 14 日美國證監會批准的納斯達克新規。主要業務在中國內地、香港或澳門的公司，如要透過 IPO 在納斯達克上市，必須以全額包銷方式募資不少於 2,500 萬美元。這條線不只看註冊地，總部和業務實際所在地也會被納入考慮。過去不少中概新股只募資幾百萬美元，這條路從此基本上行不通。

### 大門收窄了，但沒有關上

很多人把這件事讀成「美國不歡迎中國公司」。我的看法稍有不同：門檻針對的不是「中國」，而是「太小」。納斯達克的理由是，發行規模小、流通量低的股票較易成為操縱對象。

今年成功上市的例子也印證了這一點：6 月登陸納斯達克的一間中概企業募得約 5,100 萬美元，8 月在紐交所上市的一間深圳企業募得約 9,400 萬美元，兩者都遠高於新門檻。

所以，赴美上市並未關門，只是由「達到最低條件就可以試」，變成「規模和質素都要達標」。對中小企業來說，我認為有三個方向值得認真考慮。

### 一、先做大，再上市

如果公司現在只能支撐幾百萬美元的募資，硬闖上市也未必是好事。過去不少小型中概股上市後股價劇烈波動、流動性不足，結果既估值偏低，又要承擔高昂的合規成本。用一至兩年將收入、利潤和故事做大，可能是更有價值的選擇。

### 二、重新比較市場

納斯達克並非唯一的選擇。紐交所、紐交所美國板，以至港股主板及 GEM，各有不同的門檻、投資者結構和估值邏輯。生物科技和硬科技企業尤其應該比較：哪個市場的投資者最懂自己的行業，而不是哪個名字最響亮。

### 三、小心捷徑

門檻提高後，市場上自然會出現各種「繞道」方案，例如先在場外市場掛牌，或者買殼再轉板。但新規對轉板同樣設有流通市值要求，舊殼公司亦可能藏有未披露的債務或合規問題。任何捷徑，都應該先做足盡職調查。

### 一次篩選，也是一個提醒

這次規則改變對市場是一次篩選。對創辦人來說，它也是一個提醒：上市是融資的工具，不是創業的終點。早一點規劃募資規模、上市地點和合規路徑，比追趕任何一個窗口都重要。
