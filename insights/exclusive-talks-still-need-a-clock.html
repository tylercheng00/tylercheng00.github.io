Exclusive talks still need an end date獨家談判：沒有期限的風險

Summit weeks tempt open-ended exclusivity. Put an end date on the page before the headlines move again.峰會週最容易把「先獨家再說」寫進紙上——沒有結束日的獨家，往往不是謹慎，而是把日曆期權白送出去。

US capital markets美國資本市場18 Nov 20262026年11月18日7 min read閱讀約 7 分鐘

Diplomacy moves in public this week. In Shenzhen, the APEC CEO and business leaders summit still dominates 17–18 November coverage. Speeches on Asia-Pacific prosperity and bay-area cooperation sit next to clips of leaders from Korea, Vietnam and Canada. Markets and founders both watch the same feed: maybe the tone softens, maybe a corridor conversation becomes a process, maybe nothing prices into term sheets at all.

What does price into your process is exclusivity without a clock.

A buyer asks for exclusive talks. It can be reasonable. It can also freeze your fundraising while they wait to see how the summit week lands — and while Nvidia-era AI chip export politics, Jensen Huang headlines, and US–China capital-market nerves keep shifting the room’s mood. Open-ended exclusivity in a week like this is not caution. It is a free option on your calendar.

The document should say three things in plain language: when exclusivity ends; what the counterparty must deliver by that date (term sheet, diligence list, board process, cash confirmation); and what you may do if they miss it. If the draft has exclusivity and no clock, add the clock before you sign. Your other conversations are the leverage that makes exclusivity valuable to them. Giving that away for free teaches the room that your time is cheap.

Summit weeks create a specific trap. Someone says, “Let’s stay exclusive through the leaders’ meetings — the atmosphere is shifting.” Atmosphere is not a closing condition. If they need two more weeks because their investment committee watches CNBC clips of bay-area prosperity speeches, write “two weeks” and the deliverable. Do not write “until things clarify.” Things rarely clarify on a founder’s payroll schedule.

Another trap is tying exclusivity to geopolitics you do not control. “We’ll move once chip-export talk settles” or “once the US–China tone after APEC is clearer” sounds sophisticated. It is not a date. Export-control politics can stay in the air for months. Your exclusivity should not.

Practical hygiene for exclusive talks this week:

1. Put a hard end date on the page — calendar date, not “end of summit season”.
2. List the milestones they owe you before that date. No milestone, no extension by default.
3. Say what you may restart: other buyer conversations, a parallel process, a bridge discussion with existing holders.
4. Separate courtesy from contract. You can stay warm in the Shenzhen dinner circuit without freezing New York outreach in writing.

Founders sometimes fear that asking for a clock signals distrust. In US–China capital markets, a clock signals professionalism. Buyers who intend to move can live with a date. Buyers who want your silence while they shop the tape will dislike the date — which is useful information.

If exclusivity is already running and the summit news makes them ask for “a little more time,” treat the ask as a new negotiation. Extensions need a new end date and a new deliverable. Otherwise you have converted a bounded pause into an indefinite one, priced only by hope that diplomacy will hurry up for your round.

None of this predicts whether APEC changes listing windows or cross-border appetite. It only says that while diplomacy moves on television, your process still needs a clock on paper. Exclusivity without a date is a pause you cannot price. Put the end date on the page before the next speech cycle starts.

This is process advice for founders, not investment advice, and not a view on any government’s agenda. It is a reminder that headlines move faster than cash — and that your exclusivity clause should not move with them.

這星期外交在公開場合推進。深圳的APEC工商領導人峰會仍主導11月17至18日的報道。亞太繁榮、灣區合作的演講旁邊，是韓、越、加等領導人的片段。市場與創辦人看同一批新聞：語氣也許軟一點，走廊談話也許變成正式流程，也許甚麼都進不了條款清單。

真正會為你的流程定價的，往往不是這些畫面，而是沒有期限的獨家談判。

買家要求獨家，可以合理；也可以在他們觀望峰會如何落地時，把你的融資凍住——同時英偉達時代的人工智能芯片出口政治、黃仁勳相關新聞，以及美中資本市場的緊張情緒，仍在改變會議室氣氛。這種星期給出無限期獨家，不是謹慎，而是免費送給對方的日曆期權。

文件應用白話寫清三件事：獨家何時結束；到期前對方必須交付甚麼——條款清單、盡職清單、董事會流程、資金確認；若他們做不到，你可以做甚麼。若草案有獨家卻沒有期限，簽名前先把期限寫死。你與其他買家的對話，正是讓獨家對他們有價值的籌碼。免費送掉，等於告訴全場：你的時間很便宜。

峰會期間有一種常見陷阱。有人說：「領導人會議期間先保持獨家——氣氛在變。」氣氛不是交割條件。若他們因為投委會在看灣區繁榮演講的新聞片段而需要多兩星期，就寫明「兩星期」和交付物，而不是「等事情釐清」。事情很少按創辦人的發薪日曆釐清。

另一個陷阱，是把獨家綁上你控制不了的地緣政治。「等芯片出口討論定了再動」或「等APEC後美中語氣更清楚」聽起來老練，卻不是日期。出口管制政治可以懸着好幾個月；你的獨家不該如此。紙上應寫死結束日——日曆日期，不是「峰會季結束」；列出到期前他們欠你的里程碑，沒有里程碑則預設不延期；並寫明你可以重啟甚麼：其他買家對話、平行流程、與現有股東的過橋討論。客氣與合約也要分開——你可以在深圳晚宴圈保持溫熱，卻不必用白紙黑字凍結紐約外展。

創辦人有時怕要求期限像不信任對方。在美中資本市場，期限代表專業。打算推進的買家可以接受日期；想讓你沉默、自己慢慢逛盤的買家會討厭日期——這本身就是有用資訊。

若獨家已在進行，而峰會新聞讓他們要求「再多一點時間」，把這要求當新一輪談判。延期需要新的結束日和新的交付物。否則你把有期限的暫停變成無限期暫停，定價全靠希望外交會為你這輪融資趕工。

以上都不預測APEC會否改變上市窗口或跨境胃口。電視上的外交在動時，你的流程仍要紙上的期限。沒有日期的獨家，是你定不了價的暫停。下一輪演講週期開始前，先把結束日寫進文件。

這是給創辦人的流程建議，不是投資建議，也不是對任何政府議程的看法。新聞比現金快——而你的獨家條款不該跟着新聞跑。
