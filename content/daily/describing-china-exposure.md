::: en
Founders from Hong Kong and the mainland sometimes arrive in a US meeting ready for a debate about politics. The meeting is rarely that. The reader has a narrower job. They are trying to understand which parts of this company depend on rules, people, data and money inside China, and what happens to the shares they would buy if one of those dependencies shifts.

If you do not describe that, they will describe it from the last company they saw. You will not like the description.

### Make a map, not a speech

One page. Where the customers are. Where the employees are. Where the bank accounts are. Where the intellectual property is owned. Where the data sits. Which permissions the operating company needs in order to do the thing the deck says it does. Arrows for the money and arrows for the ownership, and they are not the same arrows.

A speech about globalisation does not answer the page. Neither does a speech about how unfair the questions feel. The page is not a verdict. It is a diagram a new partner inside the fund can read without you.

### Separate what is ordinary from what is structural

A company that sells to Chinese customers and employs people in Shenzhen has ordinary operating exposure, the way a German company selling in Germany does. A company whose foreign investors do not own the operating assets, and rely on contracts to capture the economics, has a structural exposure. Both can be described calmly. Mixing them into one cloud called "China risk" makes the ordinary look frightening and the structural look vague. Split them. Investors can price a split. They discount a cloud.

### Say what would change your map

Name the dependency you actually watch. A licence that renews on a date. A rule about cross-border data. A customer that is a state-owned enterprise with a procurement cycle you do not control. Then say what you would do in the year after that dependency disappointed you. Not a fantasy of instant relocation. The real first move: which entity still has cash, which contracts still pay, which people you can still employ. A reader who hears a concrete first move trusts the map more, even if they still dislike the risk.

### Do not outsource the paragraph

Counsel will draft the formal risk factor, and they should. The spoken version has to be the founder's. If the founder says "our lawyers are comfortable" and cannot draw the page, the room hears that the founder has not sat with the question. Comfort from counsel is a view about the paper. The meeting is about whether you know what the paper is describing. Those are different skills, and the investor is buying both.

Describe your own exposure in the first meeting, in your own diagram. You will still be questioned. You will be questioned about your company, which is the only version of the conversation worth having.

來自香港和內地的創辦人，有時帶着準備好跟美國人辯論政治的心情走進會議。會議很少是那樣。讀者的工作更窄。他們在弄清：這家公司的哪些部分依賴中國境內的規則、人、數據和錢；如果其中一項依賴挪動，他們要買的股份會怎樣。

如果你不描述，他們會用上一家他們看過的公司來描述。你不會喜歡那個描述。

### 做一張圖，不要做一篇演講

一頁。客戶在哪裏。僱員在哪裏。銀行賬戶在哪裏。知識產權歸誰。數據坐在哪裏。營運公司需要哪些許可，才能做簡報說它在做的事。錢的箭嘴和所有權的箭嘴，而且它們不是同一組箭嘴。

一篇關於全球化的演講回答不了這一頁。一篇關於這些問題有多不公平的演講也回答不了。這一頁不是判決。它是基金裏一個新的合夥人可以不靠你就讀懂的圖。

### 把普通的和結構性的分開

一家向中國客戶銷售、在深圳僱人的公司，有普通的營運暴露，就像一家在德國銷售的德國公司。一家外國投資者並不擁有營運資產、要靠合約去取得經濟利益的公司，有結構性的暴露。兩者都可以平靜地描述。把它們混進一團叫「中國風險」的雲，會讓普通的看起來可怕，讓結構性的看起來含糊。分開。投資者可以為分開的東西定價。他們會把一團雲打折。

### 說甚麼會改變你的圖

點出你真正在看的那項依賴。一個在某個日期續期的牌照。一條關於跨境數據的規則。一個你控制不了其採購周期的國有企業客戶。然後說，如果這項依賴令你失望，之後的一年你會做甚麼。不是立刻搬遷的幻想。真正的第一步：哪個主體還有現金、哪些合約還在付錢、哪些人你仍然僱得到。一個聽到具體第一步的讀者，會更信任這張圖，即使他們仍然不喜歡這個風險。

### 不要把這一段外包

律師會起草正式的風險因素，也應該由他們起草。說出來的版本必須是創辦人的。如果創辦人說「我們的律師是放心的」，卻畫不出這一頁，房間聽到的是：創辦人沒有跟這個問題坐在一起。律師的放心是對紙張的看法。會議是關於你是否知道紙張在描述甚麼。這是不同的能力，而投資者買的是兩者。

在第一次會議，用你自己的圖，描述你自己的暴露。你仍然會被質問。你會被質問的是你的公司。那是唯一值得進行的版本。
