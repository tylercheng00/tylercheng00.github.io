::: en
A strategic investor arrives with a logo, a possible customer, and a feeling that the round is about the relationship rather than the paper. Founders relax the terms because the money feels friendly. The friendliness may be real. The paper is still the part that will be read in three years by someone who was not in the room and does not remember the dinner.

Strategic money is ordinary money plus a set of rights that can shape the company. Price those rights on purpose.

### Separate the cheque from the commercial deal

The investment and the partnership are two contracts, even when one introduction produced both. If the corporate wants a right of first refusal on a licence, a board seat, information rights that reach into customer lists, or a veto on a sale to its competitor, that is not "being supportive". That is the commercial deal, wearing the investment as a hat.

Write the commercial terms in a document that would still make sense if the investment were zero. Then ask whether you would sign that document for the distribution, the data, or the purchase order alone. If you would not, do not sign it because a cheque is stapled to the front.

### Exclusivity is a product you are selling

A period in which you may not talk to the strategic's competitors is a real asset, and you are the one giving it away. Name its length. Name what they must do during it — a paid pilot, a named person's time, a decision date — or it is a gift. A strategic who needs twelve months of silence and offers a small cheque plus "visibility" is buying an option on your company with your own scarcity. You can sell that option. You should know the price.

### Information rights have a boundary

Corporates ask for more information than a financial investor, because they can use it in their own business. Draw the line before the first board pack. What they see as a shareholder. What they never see: other customers' pricing, a competing programme, the data of a clinical partner who did not agree to this. If the line is not written, it will be tested by a well-meaning business-development person who simply asks.

### Friendly is not the same as aligned

The strategic can be warm and still want a different outcome from yours. They may prefer you stay private and supply them. You may need to sell the company, or sell to their rival, to make the technology matter. Neither of you is wicked for this. The term sheet is where that possible divergence gets a date and a signature. Read it as if the friendship has gone quiet, because one day the people on both sides will have changed jobs, and the document will not have changed with them.

::: zh
策略投資者帶着一個標誌、一個可能的客戶，以及一種感覺：這一輪是關於關係，而不是關於紙。創辦人把條款放鬆，因為這筆錢感覺友善。友善也許是真的。紙仍然是三年後會被閱讀的部分，而讀的人當時不在房間裏，也不記得那頓飯。

策略資金是普通的錢，加上一組可以塑造公司的權利。這些權利要有意識地定價。

### 把支票和商業交易分開

投資和合作是兩份合約，即使兩者來自同一次介紹。如果這家企業要一項授權的優先權、一個董事會席位、伸進客戶名單的資訊權，或者出售給它的競爭對手時的否決權，那不是「支持」。那是商業交易，戴着投資的帽子。

把商業條款寫成一份即使投資額是零也仍然說得通的文件。然後問：如果只為了渠道、數據或訂單，你會不會簽這份文件。如果不會，就不要因為前面釘了一張支票而簽。

### 排他是你正在出售的一件產品

一段你不可以和這家策略方的競爭對手談話的時間，是真實的資產，而且是你在送出。寫下它的長度。寫下他們在這段時間內必須做的事——一個付費試驗、一個被點名的人的時間、一個決定的日期——否則它就是一份禮物。一個需要十二個月沉默、只出一張小支票加上「曝光」的策略方，是在用你自己的稀缺，買你家公司的一個期權。你可以賣這個期權。你應該知道價格。

### 資訊權要有邊界

企業要的資訊比財務投資者多，因為他們可以用在自己的業務裏。在第一份董事會文件之前把線畫好。他們作為股東看到甚麼。他們永遠看不到甚麼：其他客戶的價格、一個競爭中的項目、一位沒有同意這件事的臨床夥伴的數據。如果線沒有寫下來，它會被一個善意的業務發展人員試驗，而那個人只是開口問了。

### 友善和利益一致不是同一件事

策略方可以很溫暖，卻仍然想要一個跟你不同的結果。他們也許希望你保持私有、繼續供應他們。你也許需要把公司賣掉，或賣給他們的對手，技術才有意義。雙方都不會因此邪惡。條款清單是這種可能的分歧拿到日期和簽名的地方。把它讀成友誼已經安靜下來的樣子，因為有一天兩邊的人都換了工作，而文件不會跟着換。
