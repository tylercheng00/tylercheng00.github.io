A banker who only wants the deck is not doing diligence只想要簡報的銀行家：觀察試探與真流程

APEC and Greater Bay Area chatter create deal-flow fishing. A real US process asks for China-exposure files, not another slide polish.貿易週趕案時，真正的美國流程要中國風險檔案，不是再拋光一輪簡報。

US capital markets美國資本市場17 Nov 20262026年11月17日7 min read閱讀約 7 分鐘

Send the deck can be the start of work. It can also be a way to collect deal flow without committing attention. That second use proliferates this week: APEC business leaders open in Shenzhen, Asia-Pacific trade and investment talk elevates China’s rise and Greater Bay Area narratives, and every inbox fills with people who “want to stay close to the opportunity.” Separately, Jensen Huang, Nvidia, and AI chip-to-China control politics remind US readers that China-linked names carry diligence weight. Fishing expeditions ask for slides and time. Real processes ask for files.

Watch what happens after you send the deck. Do they ask for the cap table, the latest financials, the China-exposure paragraph, related-party schedule, IP ownership map — or do they go quiet until they want another version timed for a summit side meeting? Answer serious requests with a short index of the folder, not only with prettier slides. If the other side never opens the folder, you learned something cheaply. Protect your calendar. A process that never requests documents is not a process. It is a mailing list dressed for APEC week.

China-linked founders seeking US capital should treat the document ask as the filter. Soft interest spikes when regional investment headlines are friendly. Soft interest also spikes when bankers need to show activity to their own coverage committees. Neither spike is a mandate. A mandate starts when someone names a diligence path, a partner who can say yes, and a date for written feedback. If the only ask is a refresh of the logo wall or a one-pager for “internal circulation,” you are inventory. Inventory does not get priced; it gets sorted.

Build a response kit so fishing trips cost you minutes, not days. One current deck. One two-page teaser. One data-room index with ten to fifteen folders labelled plainly. One China-operating diagram: customers, people, cash, licences, IP. When a serious banker asks, send the index within a day and invite specific pulls. When a tourist asks only for the deck again, send the teaser once and stop iterating aesthetics. Summit weeks reward people who look busy polishing. Financing rewards people who look ready to be audited.

Watch how Nvidia politics shows up in the conversation. Some bankers will use chip-control headlines to sound sophisticated while still requesting nothing but slides. Sophistication without document requests is theatre. Others will ask early about export-adjacent supply chains, data location, and entity maps because their US accounts will ask the same. Prefer the second group even when their tone is cooler than the APEC-optimism crowd. Cool questions with a folder path attached are how processes begin. Warm compliments with no path are how weeks disappear.

Capital-markets implication is about scarcity of founder time. You cannot take every “quick sync” while Shenzhen hosts leaders. Rank counterparties by whether they have opened a file, returned written questions, or introduced a decision-maker. Demote anyone whose only artefact is your own deck with their logo added for an internal update. Promote anyone who returns a redline, a diligence list, or a dated committee slot. Ranking is not rudeness. It is how you avoid mistaking Asia-Pacific investment atmosphere for a US financing process.

If you already sent five versions to someone who never asked for the folder, stop. Send one sentence: happy to continue when you are ready for the data-room index; here it is. Then work on the company. If they engage the index, you have a process. If they ask for version six of the slides, you have your answer. Either outcome is clearer than another weekend of typography.

Keep the rule short enough to repeat in a Hong Kong morning meeting. Real process asks for files. Fishing asks for slides and calendar. In a week when trade-summit optics and chip-control politics both amplify noise around China-linked companies, the founders who stay solvent on attention are the ones who make document requests the price of another meeting. No investment advice. No claim that any banker in your inbox is acting in bad faith. Just the observable: if they never open the folder, they are not doing diligence — and your deck is not a substitute for the work only a folder can hold.

「把簡報寄來」，可以是工作的開始，也可以是收案卻不花心思的辦法。本週後一種特別多：APEC 工商領袖在深圳開幕，亞太貿易與投資話題抬高中國崛起與大灣區敘事，收件箱塞滿想「靠近機會」的人。另一邊，黃仁勳、英偉達與對華人工智能芯片管制政治提醒美國讀者：與中國相關的名字，盡職負擔不輕。試探只要投影片和時間；真流程要檔案。

看你寄出之後發生甚麼。他們是要股權表、最新財務、中國風險段落、關聯方附表、知識產權歸屬圖——還是安靜到又想要配合峰會邊會的另一版？回應認真要求時，給資料夾短目錄，不只給更好看的投影片。若對方從不打開資料夾，你便宜地學到一件事。保護你的日曆。從不要求文件的「流程」不是流程——那是穿了 APEC 週衣服的郵寄名單。

尋求美國資本、又與中國有關的創辦人，應把文件要求當成過濾器。區域投資標題友善時，軟興趣會衝高；銀行家需要向自己的覆蓋委員會展示活動時，軟興趣也會衝高。兩種衝高都不是授權。授權始於有人具名盡職路徑、能說「同意」的合夥人，以及書面反饋的日期。若唯一要求是刷新客戶商標牆，或一頁紙供「內部傳閱」，你是庫存。庫存不被定價，只被分揀。

做一個回應套件，讓試探只花你幾分鐘，不是幾天：一份當前簡報；一份兩頁引子；一份十到十五個資料夾、標籤清楚的資料室目錄；一張中國營運圖——客戶、人、現金、牌照、知識產權。認真的銀行家來問時，一天內寄目錄並邀請具體調閱。遊客只想再要簡報時，引子寄一次，停止迭代美學。峰會週獎勵看起來忙着拋光的人；融資獎勵看起來準備好被審計的人。

留意英偉達政治如何進對話。有些銀行家會用芯片管制標題顯得老練，卻仍只要求投影片——沒有文件要求的老練是演戲。另一些會早問出口相鄰供應鏈、數據位置與主體圖，因為他們的美國客戶會問同樣的事。即使語氣比 APEC 樂觀人群冷，也更偏好第二組。附帶資料夾路徑的冷靜問題，是流程的開始；沒有路徑的溫暖稱讚，是星期消失的方式。

對資本市場來說，這關乎創辦人時間稀缺。深圳接待領袖時，你無法接受每一個「快速同步」。按對方是否打開過檔案、是否回過書面問題、是否介紹過決策者來排名。任何唯一產物是「你的簡報加上他們商標作內部更新」的人，降級；任何回紅線修改、盡職清單或帶日期委員會時段的人，升級。排名不是無禮——它是避免把亞太投資氣氛誤認成美國融資流程的方法。

若你已給從不要求資料夾的人寄了五個版本，停。寄一句：你們準備好看資料室目錄時樂意繼續；目錄在此。然後去做公司的事。若他們接目錄，你有流程；若他們要第六版投影片，你有答案。兩種結果都比又一個週末調字體清楚。

規則短到能在香港早晨會議重複：真流程要檔案；試探要投影片和日曆。在貿易峰會畫面與芯片管制政治同時放大中國相關公司噪音的一週，注意力仍保得住的創辦人，是讓文件要求成為下次會議代價的人。沒有投資建議，也不宣稱收件箱裏任何銀行家存心不良。只看可觀察的：若他們從不打開資料夾，他們不是在做盡職調查——而你的簡報，替代不了只有資料夾裝得下的工作。
