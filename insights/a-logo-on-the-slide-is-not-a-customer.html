A logo on the slide is not a customer觀察：簡報上的商標牆，並非客戶

Shenzhen’s business-leaders summit will amplify Greater Bay Area and China-rise stories. US capital still prices paid invoices, not logo walls.峰會週增長敘事再響，美國投資人仍問：誰付了錢、買了甚麼。

US capital markets美國資本市場17 Nov 20262026年11月17日7 min read閱讀約 7 分鐘

This week from Hong Kong, APEC business leaders open in Shenzhen, and Asia-Pacific trade and investment talk puts China’s economic rise and the Greater Bay Area back on every screen. Separately, Jensen Huang and Nvidia sit in US headlines over AI chip sales into China, with bipartisan pressure to tighten controls. That mix tempts China-linked founders to lead with growth optics: partner logos, pilot badges, “ecosystem” walls that look like demand. Optics are not customers. A US diligence room reading summit optimism next to export-control politics will still ask what was paid, for what, and by whom.

Logo walls are easy to build and hard to defend. A pilot, an unpaid pilot, a letter of intent, and a paid contract are four different objects. Investors who have been burned will ask which one you mean within two minutes. In a week when capital markets are celebrating regional investment narratives, the temptation is to let the wall do the talking. The wall cannot answer cancellation rights, invoice dates, or whether the logo belongs to a free evaluation seat. Those answers decide whether your growth story survives a partner who just skimmed chip-restriction coverage and is already sceptical of China-touching revenue quality.

Write the relationship in one sentence on the appendix page, not only in the wall. Paid since which month. What they buy. Whether they can cancel in thirty days. Currency and counterparty entity. If the buyer sits in the mainland, Hong Kong, or elsewhere, say so plainly — the same week people are debating who can buy advanced chips is a week when “Asia customer” as a single bucket looks careless. If the answer is they love us with no invoice, the logo is branding, not revenue proof. Branding has a place. It does not belong in the line that supports a US financing mark.

You can still show early users. Call them early users. Accuracy here saves a diligence call later, when someone checks the bank and finds the story thinner than the slide. Summit-week optimism does not fill that gap. Neither does a Greater Bay Area keynote that makes every regional logo feel inevitable. Inevitable is not collected cash. Collected cash is a date, an amount, and a contract that still binds after the cameras leave Shenzhen.

Build a customer evidence pack before you refresh the deck for APEC-adjacent meetings. One table: logo, stage (pilot / LOI / paid), first invoice date, last invoice date, trailing twelve-month cash if any, notice period, concentration risk. One folder path to the three contracts that matter most. One sentence on what would break if a large mainland or regional buyer paused procurement because trade politics cooled the mood. That sentence is not panic. It is how you show you have thought about the same news the investor is reading.

Capital implications for China-linked founders seeking US money are straightforward. US readers will mix fair commercial questions with mood questions about control risk and supply chains. Answer the commercial questions with paper. Do not use the logo wall as a substitute for paper because the summit week makes China growth feel fashionable again. Fashionable logos are how decks get meetings. Paid, named, cancellable-or-not relationships are how meetings become processes. If a banker asks only for a prettier wall this week, treat that as a fishing trip, not as validation of your revenue.

Watch concentration. One logo that is eighty percent of “traction” is a single point of failure dressed as a partnership. In a loud news cycle about US–China tech boundaries, concentration in a politically sensitive vertical gets priced worse, not better. Diversify the evidence or shrink the claim. Either is cleaner than a wall of names that collapse into one payment stream under diligence.

Keep the rule boring on purpose. No invented customer theatre. No claim that APEC attendance predicts your close. Just the hygiene that works when Shenzhen hosts leaders and when Washington argues about Nvidia: if you cannot say what was paid, for what, and by whom, remove the logo until you can. Early users stay on the page with honest labels. Paying customers stay with dates. Everything else is atmosphere — and atmosphere is what this week already has enough of.

現象觀察：APEC工商領袖峰會在深圳開幕，「中國經濟崛起」與粵港澳大灣區又被推上螢幕；另一邊，黃仁勳與英偉達因對華人工智能芯片銷售佔滿美國新聞，兩黨都在施壓收緊管制。兩邊夾着，與中國有關的創辦人容易用「看起來像增長」的畫面開場——夥伴商標、試點徽章、密密麻麻的「生態系」牆。問題很簡單：這些畫面不是客戶。美國投資人做盡職調查時，一邊看峰會樂觀氣氛，一邊看出口管制政治，仍會問三件事：付了甚麼、買甚麼、誰付的。

商標牆好建，難辯護。試點、未付費試點、意向書、已簽且已付款的合同，是四種完全不同的關係。吃過虧的投資人，兩分鐘內就會追問你指的是哪一種。資本市場在慶祝區域投資故事的一週，人很容易讓牆替自己說話。可牆回答不了取消權、發票日期，也說不清那個商標是不是只佔了一個免費試用席。這些細節，才決定你的增長故事能否過關——尤其對方剛掃過芯片限制報道，已對「與中國有關」的收入質量存疑。

與其只靠牆，不如在附錄用一句話寫清楚關係：從哪個月開始付費、買的是甚麼、能否三十天取消、用甚麼貨幣、簽約主體是誰。買家在內地、香港還是其他地方，直接說——人們在辯論誰能買先進芯片的同一週，把「亞洲客戶」籠統歸成一類，顯得粗心。若答案是「他們很喜歡我們」卻沒有發票，商標只是品牌，不是收入證明。品牌有位置，但不該出現在支撐美國融資估值的那一行。

早期用戶當然可以展示，就老實叫早期用戶。這裏寫準，能省掉後來有人查銀行流水、發現故事比投影片瘦的那通電話。峰會週的樂觀填不了缺口；讓每個區域商標都顯得「勢在必得」的大灣區演講，也填不了。勢在必得不是收到的現金。現金是日期、金額，以及鏡頭離開深圳之後仍約束雙方的合同。

為與APEC相關的會議刷新簡報之前，先做一份客戶證據包。一張表：商標、階段（試點／意向／已付費）、首張與最近發票日、若有則過去十二個月實收現金、通知期、收入是否過度集中於單一客戶。一條通往最重要三份合同的資料夾路徑。再加一句：若大型內地或區域買家因貿易政治冷卻而暫停採購，甚麼會斷。那句話不是恐慌，而是表明你想過投資人正在讀的同一則新聞。

對尋求美國資金、又與中國有關的創辦人來說，含義很直接。美國讀者會把公平的商業問題，和管制風險、供應鏈的情緒問題混在一起。商業問題用文件回答；不要因為峰會週又讓「中國增長」顯得時髦，就用商標牆代替文件。時髦的商標能換會議；已付費、具名、可否取消說得清的關係，才能讓會議變成正式流程。若銀行家這個星期只想要更好看的牆，把它當成試探，不是對你收入的確認。

也要留意收入是否過度集中。一個佔「牽引數據」八成的商標，不過是打扮成夥伴關係的單一依賴。在美中科技邊界嘈雜的新聞周期裏，落在政治敏感行業的集中度會被看得更差，不是更好。要麼分散證據，要麼縮小主張——兩者都比在盡職調查下一塌成一條付款流的名字牆乾淨。

規則刻意保持無聊：不編造客戶劇場，不宣稱參加APEC就能預測交割。只堅持深圳接待領袖、華盛頓爭論英偉達時都管用的習慣——若說不出付了甚麼、為甚麼、誰付的，先拿掉商標。早期用戶留在頁上，標籤誠實；付費客戶留下日期。其餘都是氣氛，而這個星期氣氛已經夠多了。
