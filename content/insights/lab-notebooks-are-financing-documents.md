::: en
Scientists often treat notebooks as private memory. Investors and partners treat them as evidence. Who ran the assay. Which lot of reagent. Whether the protocol matched the one in the deck. Gaps here become ownership fights and reproducibility fights later.

You do not need a perfect electronic system on day one. You need dated entries, named operators, and a way to find the run behind a chart. When a diligence team asks whether they can see the raw, the answer should be a path, not a scramble.

Put notebook hygiene on the same list as assignments and patents. It is less glamorous. It is often what lets the glamorous slide survive a second look.

::: zh
科學家常把筆記本當成私人記憶。投資者和夥伴把它當成證據。誰跑了檢測。哪一批試劑。規程是否和投影片一致。缺口後來會變成權屬之爭和可重現性之爭。

第一天不需要完美的電子系統。你需要有日期的記錄、具名的操作者、以及找到圖表背後那次實驗的方法。盡職審查問能否看原始數據時，答案應是一條路徑，不是一場翻箱。

把筆記本紀律和轉讓、專利放在同一清單。比較不華麗。卻常常讓華麗的那頁能經得起第二次看。
