::: en
A founder in Hong Kong sends a revised model at 7pm, which is 6am in New York, and feels the day was productive. New York reads it at 9am their time, has questions by lunch, and sends them at 4pm, which is 5am in Hong Kong. The founder sees the questions the next morning and answers that night. Two calendar days have passed, and one round trip has occurred.

This is not bad luck. It is the arithmetic of the route, and it will dominate the round if you do not design for it.

### Overlap is a meeting, not a mood

There is a thin window in which both sides are properly awake. Treat it as scarce inventory. Use it for decisions and for questions that need a voice. Do not use it to read a document together that one side could have read alone. Send the document the evening before, with the three questions you actually need answered in the overlap, written at the top. The call then starts at the decision instead of at "can everyone see the screen".

If you have no overlap because someone is travelling, say so and move the decision. Pretending a tired midnight call is a working session produces agreements that unravel at the lawyers the next day.

### One bundle a day, not a stream

Five emails across the Hong Kong afternoon arrive in New York as a pile at breakfast, out of order, the latest contradicting the first. Send one bundle, at a stated time, with a list of what changed. The other side can then do one piece of work instead of reconstructing your afternoon. This feels slower to the sender. It is faster for the round, because nothing is answered twice.

I like a named cutoff. "Anything after 4pm Hong Kong waits for tomorrow's bundle." People hate this until they see a week in which nothing was lost.

### Write for the person who is not on the call

Half the readers will see your work without you. Number the questions. State the ask in the first three lines. Attach the file whose name matches the file you mention. Mark what is new in colour or in a note, so nobody re-reads twenty pages to find a changed cell. Courtesy here is not softness. It is how you buy a same-day reply from someone whose day is already ending.

### Give one person the clock

Someone should own the timezone. Their job is to know which items are waiting on New York, which are waiting on Hong Kong, and which have been waiting so long that the wait itself is now the issue. A shared list, updated once a day, beats a hero founder who answers everything personally at 1am and becomes the bottleneck they were trying to avoid.

The ocean is not going to shrink. The round can still move every business day, if you stop asking a single email to cross it twice before anything is decided.

::: zh
香港的創辦人晚上七時送出修訂的模型，那時紐約是早上六時，他覺得這一天很有產出。紐約在當地九時讀到，午餐前有了問題，下午四時送出，那時香港是凌晨五時。創辦人第二天早上看到問題，當晚回答。兩個曆日過去了，只完成了一個來回。

這不是運氣不好。這是這條路線的算術。如果你不為它設計，它會支配這一輪。

### 重疊的時間是一次會議，不是一種心情

雙方都真正醒着的窗口很薄。把它當成稀缺的庫存。用來做決定，和用來問那些需要聲音的問題。不要用它來一起讀一份一方本來可以自己讀的文件。在重疊之前的那個晚上把文件送出，把你真正需要在重疊裏回答的三個問題寫在最上面。電話就從決定開始，而不是從「大家看得到螢幕嗎」開始。

如果因為有人在途中而沒有重疊，就說出來，並把決定挪走。假裝一次疲倦的午夜電話是工作會議，會產生第二天在律師那裏散掉的協議。

### 一天一捆，而不是一條溪流

香港下午的五封電郵，到紐約是早餐時的一堆，次序亂了，最新的一封否定第一封。在一個說好的時間送出一捆，並列出改了甚麼。對方就可以做一件工作，而不是重建你的下午。對發送的人來說這感覺更慢。對這一輪來說它更快，因為沒有事情被回答兩次。

我喜歡一個有名字的截止。「香港下午四時之後的東西，等明天的那一捆。」人們討厭這個，直到他們看到一個甚麼都沒有弄丟的星期。

### 為不在電話上的那個人寫

一半的讀者會在沒有你的情況下看你的工作。把問題編號。在頭三行寫明你要甚麼。附件的檔名要和你提到的檔名一致。用顏色或一句話標出新的部分，好讓沒有人為了找一個改過的儲存格而重讀二十頁。這裏的禮貌不是軟弱。它是你向一個白天已經要結束的人，買一個當天回覆的方式。

### 讓一個人掌管時鐘

應該有人負責時區。他的工作是知道哪些項目在等紐約、哪些在等香港、哪些等得太久、久到等待本身成了問題。一張每天更新一次的共用清單，好過一個凌晨一時親自回答一切、然後變成自己想避免的瓶頸的英雄創辦人。

海洋不會縮小。只要你不再要求一封電郵在任何決定之前跨越它兩次，這一輪仍然可以每個工作日都往前。
