IPO, SPAC or M&A? Three questions to ask before you pick a routeIPO、SPAC 還是併購？選擇路線前先問三個問題

The route to the public markets should follow from what the company needs, not from what is fashionable this year.走哪條路進入資本市場，應該由公司的需要決定，而不是由今年流行甚麼決定。

US capital markets美國資本市場4 Nov 20262026年11月4日3 min read閱讀約 3 分鐘

Every few years a different route to the US market becomes fashionable. For a while everyone wanted a SPAC. Then nobody did. I think the better approach is to ignore the fashion and start with three plain questions.

1. Do you need new money, or do you need liquidity?

A traditional IPO raises new capital for the company. A direct listing mainly gives existing shareholders a way to sell. If the business needs cash to grow, the answer already points somewhere. If the founders and early investors mainly want a market for their shares, it points somewhere else.

2. Can your numbers live in public, every quarter?

Being listed means reporting, explaining misses, and having analysts and short sellers read your filings. Some businesses are lumpy by nature — a deep-tech company with a few large contracts a year, for example. That is not a reason never to list. It is a reason to be honest about whether the public market will reward you, or just punish the lumpy quarters.

3. Who already values what you do?

This is the one people skip. Sometimes public investors are the natural buyers of your story. But sometimes a strategic acquirer will value your patents, team or customer base far more than the market would. For science-based companies especially, I think M&A is underrated as a route — not a failure to list, but a different way to get the right owner for the asset.

On SPACs

SPACs are not good or bad in themselves. They are a negotiated deal with a listed shell, which gives some certainty on valuation. But redemptions can shrink the cash you actually receive, and regulators have tightened the rules. Treat a SPAC as one option to price against the others, not as a shortcut.

Answer those three questions first. The route usually becomes obvious.

每隔幾年，就會有一條進入美國市場的路線變得流行。有一段時間人人都想做 SPAC，後來又沒有人想做。我認為更好的做法，是不理會潮流，先問三個簡單的問題。

1. 你需要的是新資金，還是流動性？

傳統 IPO 會為公司募集新資金。直接上市主要是讓現有股東有渠道出售股份。如果業務需要資金增長，答案已經指向一個方向；如果創辦人和早期投資者主要想為手上的股份找一個市場，答案就指向另一個方向。

2. 你的數字能否每一季都攤在公眾面前？

上市代表要定期披露、要解釋為何未達標，還要讓分析師和沽空者細讀你的文件。有些業務天生就起伏大，例如一年只有幾張大合約的深科技公司。這不代表永遠不應上市，但要誠實地問：公開市場會欣賞你，還是只會懲罰那些起伏的季度？

3. 誰已經認同你在做的事？

這是最常被忽略的問題。有時公開市場的投資者確實是你故事的天然買家；但有時，一個策略收購方對你的專利、團隊或客戶的估值，會遠高於市場。尤其是科研型公司，我認為併購是被低估的路線。它不是上市失敗，而是為這項資產找到合適主人的另一種方式。

關於 SPAC

SPAC 本身無所謂好壞。它是與一家已上市空殼公司協商的交易，令估值較有確定性。但贖回可能令你實際收到的資金大減，監管規則亦已收緊。把 SPAC 視為其中一個選項，與其他路線比較定價，而不是捷徑。

先回答這三個問題，路線通常就會變得很清楚。
