::: en
Hong Kong desks spent 20 November watching a rare tape: Anthropic’s stock-market debut as a pure AI public company, while US Commerce had just approved large AI-chip sales to Chinese firms and Congress kept pressing for tighter controls after Jensen Huang and Nvidia scrutiny. Valuation chatter travels faster than diligence. Founders with open private rounds will hear the same sentence in three languages before lunch: “Look at the public comps.” A liquid multiple is a reference. It is not your price.

Public comps look objective because they sit on a screen with a ticker. Between that ticker and your cap table sit liquidity, scale, disclosure, history, and a buyer who can exit tomorrow. Your investor cannot. Pretending those gaps are footnotes is how a comps page becomes a credibility problem. Use the public name to show the category people already understand. Then say, in one bridge paragraph, what would have to be true for your near-term number to earn a fraction of that multiple — revenue quality, gross margin path, concentration risk, regulatory exposure, and how soon you can produce audited numbers a stranger trusts.

Anthropic’s day is useful precisely because it will flood decks with AI multiples that feel newly “market.” Markets are noisy on debut days. Opening prints, peer reactions, and analyst notes are not a private-market clearing price for a Series B in Shenzhen or a growth round in Hong Kong. They are atmosphere. Atmosphere belongs in the news tab. Your price belongs in a sentence that names cash need, runway in weeks, and the milestones the new money must buy. If you cannot write that sentence without citing a public ticker, you are negotiating someone else’s story.

Chip politics makes the comps trap sharper this week. US approvals for large AI-chip sales into China sit beside continued congressional pressure for tighter controls. That split trains public investors to reprice AI supply chains hour by hour. Private companies that sell tools, models, or biotech workflows with China compute exposure will be asked to map that exposure anyway. Do not answer by waving a public AI multiple. Answer with an operating map: where inference runs, which vendors you depend on, what breaks if export rules tighten again, and what customer contracts already assume. The map is diligence. The multiple is decoration.

Buyers who know the space discount a lazy comps page in minutes. They have seen the same slide every cycle: a logo row, a median EV/revenue, a circled “implied valuation,” and a claim that you deserve the top quartile because the category is hot. Hot categories still clear on specifics. Prefer a humble bridge. Today’s revenue or contracted backlog. Today’s burn. The twelve-month proof that would justify a higher multiple later. The governance and disclosure steps between private opacity and public readiness. That bridge tells the room you understand the difference between a debut narrative and a financing fact.

Founders raising while AI names dominate the tape should also decide what not to update. Do not refresh the comps page every hour to chase the debut print. Do not WhatsApp LPs a screenshot of someone else’s first-day move as if it were a term-sheet argument. Do send a short note if your own metrics moved — a signed pilot, a completed audit step, a cleared counsel memo on China exposure. Your process should track your company, not Anthropic’s opening auction.

None of this is advice to ignore public markets. Public markets set the language partners already speak. Speak it carefully. Say which public companies define the category and why. Say which differences matter for risk. Say what multiple you are not claiming. Then put your ask in cash and months, not in a borrowed EV/sales ratio. On a day when a pure AI company is newly public and chip policy is still contested, the founders who sound adult are the ones who refuse to confuse a headline multiple with a closed round.

If a banker insists the comps page stays, keep it, but demote it. One slide for category context. One slide for your bridge. One slide for use of proceeds in weeks. The order teaches the room how to read you. Category first, rights second, is how private processes quietly fail after public debuts. Rights first, category second, is how you stay in control of your own price talk while the tape does whatever the tape does.

十一月二十日，香港交易枱難得同時盯兩條線：Anthropic以「純人工智能」公眾公司姿態上市，估值討論鋪天蓋地；另一邊，美國商務部剛批准對中國企業的大額人工智能芯片銷售，國會卻仍在英偉達與黃仁勳受關注之後，推動更緊的出口管制。公開市場倍數傳得比盡職調查快。正在融資的創辦人，午飯前大概會用三種語言聽到同一句：「看看上市公司可比。」流動股份的倍數是參考，不能直接拿來當你的價錢。

可比看起來客觀，因為螢幕上有代碼。但代碼與你的股權表之間，隔着流動性、規模、信息披露、營運歷史，以及明天就能出場的買家——你的投資人通常沒有這種出場。把這些差距當註腳，可比頁就會變成可信度問題。較穩妥的用法，是借公眾名字說明市場已懂的賽道；接着用一段「橋樑」寫清：近端數字要具備甚麼，才配得上那個倍數的一小部分——收入質量、毛利率路徑、客戶集中、監管暴露，以及多久能交出陌生人信得過的經審計數字。

Anthropic上市日之所以特別容易誤導，正因為它會讓簡報瞬間灌滿「剛剛被市場證明」的人工智能倍數。首日成交、同業反應、分析師短評，並不是深圳A輪或香港成長輪的清算價，只是氣氛。氣氛是新聞；價錢是一句話：要多少現金、跑道還有幾週、新錢必須買到哪些里程碑。若離開公眾代碼就寫不出這句話，你談的其實是別人的故事。

本週芯片政治也讓「可比陷阱」更尖。美國放行大額對華人工智能芯片銷售，與國會持續收緊管制的壓力並存，公眾投資人會按小時重估供應鏈。賣工具、模型、或帶中國算力依賴的生技流程的私人公司，本來就會被要求畫出暴露圖。別用公眾人工智能倍數擋回去；用營運圖回答：推理跑在哪裏、依賴哪些供應商、出口規則再緊時甚麼會斷、客戶合約已預設甚麼。圖是盡調，倍數是裝飾。

識行的買家幾分鐘就會打折一頁懶可比。每個週期都見過同一張投影片：一排標誌、一個EV／收入中位數、一個圈起來的「隱含估值」，再聲稱賽道熱所以你該拿上四分位。熱賽道仍按細節成交。寧可做謙虛的橋：今天的收入或已簽約積壓、今天的現金消耗、未來十二個月若要支撐更高倍數需要甚麼證明，以及從私人不透明走到公眾就緒還缺哪些治理與披露。這座橋告訴房間：你分得清上市敘事與融資事實。

人工智能名稱主導行情時，也要決定甚麼不更新。不要每小時追首日成交去改可比頁；不要把別人首日截圖丟進有限合夥人通訊軟件，當成條款論據。若動的是你自己的指標——簽了的試點、完成的審計節點、過目的中國暴露律師備忘——才值得短訊同步。流程應對準自己的公司，而不是Anthropic的開盤拍賣。

這不是叫人無視公開市場。公開市場提供合夥人已在使用的語言；要小心使用。說哪些公眾公司界定賽道、為甚麼；說哪些差異構成風險；說你不主張哪個倍數；再把請求落成現金與月份，而不是借來的EV／銷售比。當純人工智能公司新上市、芯片政策仍在拉鋸，聽起來成熟的創辦人，是拒絕把頭條倍數誤認成已關閉輪次的那批人。

若銀行家堅持保留可比頁，可以留，但要降級：一頁賽道語境、一頁你的橋樑、一頁以「週」為單位的資金用途。順序本身在教房間如何讀你。先講賽道再講權利，是私人流程在公眾亮相後安靜失敗的常見路徑；先講權利再講賽道，才較能在行情自顧自波動時，守住自己的價錢談話。
