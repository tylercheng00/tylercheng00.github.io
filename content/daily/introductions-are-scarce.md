::: en
A good introduction is not a favour in the ordinary sense. The person making it is telling someone they respect: this is worth your time, and I am willing to be associated with the outcome. That stock is small. Founders often spend it as if it were free.

I would rather make one introduction that fits than five that are "worth a conversation".

### Fit before warmth

The wrong investor, warmly introduced, is worse than no introduction. They take the meeting, they pass, and the person who introduced you has now spent a chip to produce a no. The next time that person thinks of you, they remember the pass. Warmth does not repair a mismatch of stage, cheque size, or what the investor actually knows how to judge.

Before I ask anyone for an introduction, I want three sentences. Why this investor, not investors in general. What they have backed that resembles this. What we are asking them to do in this meeting, which is usually not "explore synergies". It is a decision, or a clear next step.

### Send the note yourself

The kindest thing you can do for the person introducing you is to write the email they will forward. Five or six lines. What the company does. Why now. Why this recipient. What you want. No attachments unless asked. No life story.

If the introducer has to compose your case, they will either delay, or write something vaguer than you would have written. Both are your fault. A forwardable note is part of being ready, in the same way a data room is.

### Do not ask twice for the same door

If an investor has passed, do not route a second introduction through someone else and hope the first one is forgotten. It will not be. Circles in this work are smaller than founders think, especially between Hong Kong, the mainland and the US funds that look at both. A second approach through a different friend reads as either desperation or disorganisation. Come back when something material has changed, and say what changed.

### After the meeting, close the loop

Tell the introducer what happened. Two lines are enough. They met us. They passed, for this reason. Or: they asked for the model, and we sent it on Thursday. People who are not told what happened stop introducing. Not out of spite. Out of the reasonable belief that their name went into a hole.

Treat every introduction as a withdrawal from someone else's account. Then ask whether this particular meeting is worth what it costs them.

::: zh
一次好的介紹，不是普通意義上的幫忙。做介紹的人是在對一位他尊重的人說：這值得你的時間，而且我願意跟結果連在一起。這筆存款很少。創辦人卻常常把它花得好像不用錢。

我寧願做一次合適的介紹，而不是五次「聊聊也好」。

### 先合不合適，再談熱不熱情

介紹給錯誤的投資者，即使很熱情，也比沒有介紹更差。他們見了，然後拒絕，而介紹你的人用一枚籌碼換來一個「不」。下次他想到你，記得的是這次拒絕。階段、支票大小，或者這位投資者不懂判斷的東西，對不上，熱情補不了。

我請人介紹之前，想要三句話。為甚麼是這位投資者，而不是泛指的投資者。他們投過甚麼跟這家相像的。這次會議我們要他們做甚麼，通常不是「探索協同」。是一個決定，或一個清楚的下一步。

### 電郵你自己寫

你能為介紹人做的最體貼的事，是寫好他會轉發的那封電郵。五六行。公司做甚麼。為甚麼是現在。為甚麼是這位收件人。你想要甚麼。除非對方要求，否則不要附件。不要人生故事。

如果介紹人要自己組織你的說法，他要麼會拖，要麼寫得比你會寫的更含糊。兩者都是你的問題。一封可以轉發的短信，和資料室一樣，是準備的一部分。

### 同一扇門不要敲兩次

投資者已經拒絕，不要再經另一個人介紹一次，希望第一次被忘記。不會的。這個圈子比創辦人以為的小，尤其是香港、內地，和同時看兩邊的美國基金之間。經不同的朋友第二次接觸，讀起來要麼是絕望，要麼是混亂。等有實質改變再回來，並說明改變了甚麼。

### 見完面，把圈子合上

告訴介紹人發生了甚麼。兩行就夠。我們見了。他們拒絕了，原因是這個。或者：他們要了模型，我們星期四送了。沒有人告知結果的人，會停止介紹。不是出於怨恨。是合理地相信，自己的名字掉進了一個洞。

把每一次介紹看成從別人的戶口提款。然後問：這一次會議，值不值得他們付這個代價。
