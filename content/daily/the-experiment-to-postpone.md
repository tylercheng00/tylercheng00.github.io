::: en
Science companies love the experiment that would look good in a deck. A larger animal. A harder disease model. A head-to-head against the incumbent. Those experiments have a place. They are often scheduled before the company has earned the right to interpret them, because the result, whatever it is, will not mean what the slide needs it to mean.

The experiment to postpone is the one whose answer you cannot yet use.

### Ask what you will do with each outcome

Before an experiment is booked, write two short plans. If it works, the next month looks like this. If it fails, the next month looks like this. If those two plans are the same — "we will optimise and repeat" — the experiment is not yet a decision. It is an activity. Activities can be scientifically interesting and still be the wrong use of the last batch of material, or of the last quarter of runway.

An experiment earns its place when at least one outcome would change what the company spends money on.

### The dull experiment that unlocks the glamorous one

Often the postponed experiment is waiting on something unimpressive. An assay that actually measures the thing you think it measures. A batch that matches the last batch. A dose you can explain. Running the glamorous study on top of an untrusted assay does not produce a bold result. It produces a result you will spend the following month defending, and then quietly setting aside. Do the dull work first, and put it in the plan under its own name, so nobody experiences it as a delay to the real science. It is the real science.

### Do not run it because the slot is free

A collaborator's animal facility has a window. A student is available. A reagent is about to expire. These are real constraints, and they push teams into experiments the plan did not ask for. Sometimes you should take the window. More often you should let the reagent expire. A free experiment is not free if it consumes the only people who understand the method, and returns a dataset that now has to be explained forever. Scarcity of attention is part of the budget.

### Leave a record of what you did not do

The experiments you postpone will be asked about, by a board member who saw them on an old slide. Keep a line: we did not do this, because this prior fact was missing, and we will reconsider when that fact exists. That line is more professional than a study which happened because nobody wanted to say no to a dramatic figure. Discipline in what you do not run is visible later, in the cleanliness of what you did run.

A good plan has a waiting list. A weak plan has everything in progress, and nothing that could come to a conclusion.

科研公司喜歡那個放在簡報裏會好看的實驗。更大的動物。更難的疾病模型。跟現有標準的正面比較。這些實驗有它們的位置。它們常常被排在公司還沒有資格詮釋它們之前，因為無論結果是甚麼，都不會是投影片需要它意味的那個意思。

應該押後的實驗，是那個你還用不上它的答案的實驗。

### 問每一個結果你會做甚麼

實驗排期之前，寫兩個短計劃。如果成功，下個月是這樣。如果失敗，下個月是這樣。如果兩個計劃是一樣的——「我們會優化然後重複」——這個實驗還不是一個決定。它是一項活動。活動可以在科學上有趣，卻仍然是最後一批材料、或跑道最後一季的錯誤用法。

至少有一個結果會改變公司把錢花在哪裏，這個實驗才配佔一個位置。

### 那個能解開漂亮實驗的沉悶實驗

被押後的實驗，常常在等一件不起眼的事。一個真的量到你以為它在量的東西的檢測。一批跟上一批相符的生產。一個你解釋得了的劑量。在一個不被信任的檢測上做漂亮的研究，不會產生一個大膽的結果。它產生一個你下個月要用來辯護、然後悄悄放下的結果。先做沉悶的工作，並在計劃裏用它自己的名字寫出來，好讓沒有人把它體驗成真正科學的延誤。它就是真正的科學。

### 不要因為檔期是空的就做

合作方的動物設施有一個空窗。一個學生有空。一種試劑快過期。這些是真的約束，它們推動團隊去做計劃沒有要求的實驗。有時你應該抓住空窗。更多時候你應該讓試劑過期。一個免費的實驗並不免費，如果它耗掉唯一懂這個方法的人，並交回一套從此必須永遠解釋的數據。注意力的稀缺是預算的一部分。

### 留下你沒有做的記錄

你押後的實驗，會被一位在舊投影片上看過它們的董事問到。留一行：我們沒有做這個，因為缺少這一個先前的事實，等那個事實存在時我們會再考慮。這一行，比一個因為沒有人想對一張戲劇性的圖說不而發生的研究，更專業。你沒有做的事情裏的紀律，後來會在你做過的事情的乾淨程度裏被看見。

好的計劃有一張等候名單。弱的計劃是一切都在進行，而沒有一件可以得出結論。
