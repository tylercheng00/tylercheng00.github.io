On 18 November 2026, the China story in a fundraising room is not only your story. It is also the day’s feed: APEC CEO and business leaders in Shenzhen, speeches on Asia-Pacific prosperity and bay areas, leaders from Korea, Vietnam and Canada on the same wire, and — still in the air — Jensen Huang, Nvidia, and AI chip export-control politics. Investors will hear your customer logos through that filter whether you invite the filter or not.

That is why reference calls are part of the product this week, not a nice-to-have after the science slides.

Scientific and commercial references remain different. A collaborator who co-authored a paper may not want to discuss manufacturing claims. A hospital pilot lead may not want to be named while procurement is open. A paid user in Shenzhen may be happy to talk about uptime and unwilling to talk about “China exposure” as a theme. Know which is which before you promise a call in a meeting that opened with summit small talk.

Geopolitics reframes the China story in a specific way. Prosperity and bay-area language from the summit can make a founder lean into a regional narrative. Export-control noise can make the same founder over-correct into a “we are not that kind of China company” speech. Neither speech replaces a customer who will say, on a scheduled call, what they buy, why they renew, and what would make them leave. The reference is the product proof that survives the headline cycle.

Prepare two or three people who have agreed in advance. Tell them what topics are fair: product performance, support, timeline honesty, change-control. Tell them what is off-limits if they need it off-limits: politics, chip policy, other vendors. Tell the investor what they will and will not hear. A reference that surprises everyone on the call is not diligence. It is risk — especially in a week when people are already primed to over-read every mainland signal.

If your customers are mostly in the Greater Bay Area, say so calmly and offer the call. Do not hide the geography because the room is watching APEC clips, and do not inflate the geography into a geopolitics thesis. Ordinary operating exposure — selling in China, employing in Shenzhen — can be described the way a German company describes selling in Germany. What you cannot do is promise a “global” reference set you do not have, or a US reference who has never used the product, just to balance a slide for US–China capital-markets optics.

If you have no one who will speak, say so and explain why: early stage, confidential pilots, regulated settings, customers who will not take political-adjacent questions this week. Silence with a reason beats a scramble to find a friendly voice after the meeting ends. It also beats putting a logo on a slide and hoping nobody asks for a number.

Practical checklist before your next call this week:

1. Name the humans, not only the logos. Confirm they will answer in a defined window.
2. Separate science references from commercial ones. Do not mix the lists in the data room.
3. Brief references on the news climate without scripting politics. They should expect questions coloured by summit and chip headlines; they should not be asked to perform a foreign-policy view.
4. Tell investors the limit of the call. “They will discuss product use; they will not discuss export licences” is a clean sentence.
5. Retire any “customers love us” line that cannot survive the question “who takes the call on Thursday?”

US–China capital markets will keep reading China stories through whatever the wire carries on 18 November. Your job is narrower. Make the product legible through a voice that is not yours. Summit prosperity talk changes the acoustics of the room. It does not change the need for a named customer who will pick up the phone.

This is diligence hygiene, not investment advice, and not a claim about how APEC or chip policy will settle. It is a reminder that when geopolitics reframes the China story, the reference call is still how buyers test whether the story is a product.

2026 年 11 月 18 日，融資會議室裡的「中國故事」不只是你的故事，也是當日資訊流：深圳的 APEC 工商領導人峰會、亞太繁榮與灣區演講、同一條電訊上的韓、越、加領導人，以及——仍未散去的——黃仁勳、Nvidia 與 AI 芯片出口管制政治。無論你是否主動引入這個濾鏡，投資者都會隔着它聽你的客戶 logo。

因此這星期，客戶推薦電話是產品的一部分，不是科學投影片之後的錦上添花。

科學推薦與商業推薦仍然不同。共同撰寫論文的合作者未必想談製造主張；醫院試點負責人在採購未定時未必願具名；深圳的付費用戶也許樂意談系統可用性，卻不願把「中國風險」當主題來談。在以峰會寒暄開場的會議裡承諾安排電話之前，先分清楚。

地緣政治會以特定方式改寫中國故事。峰會的繁榮與灣區語言，會讓創辦人傾向區域敘事；出口管制噪音又會讓同一個人矯枉過正，講成「我們不是那種中國公司」。兩種講法都替代不了一個客戶：在約定好的電話裡，說清楚他們買甚麼、為甚麼續約、甚麼會讓他們離開。推薦，才是撐過新聞週期的產品證明。

預先準備兩三位已同意接受訪談的人。告訴他們哪些題目合適：產品表現、支援、時間線是否誠實、變更控制。若他們需要界線，講明甚麼不談：政治、芯片政策、其他供應商。同時告訴投資者會聽到甚麼、聽不到甚麼。讓所有人措手不及的推薦不是盡職審查，是風險——尤其在人們已準備過度解讀每一個內地訊號的這個星期。

若客戶多半在大灣區，平靜地說，並提供電話。不要因為房間在看 APEC 短片就藏起地理，也不要把地理吹成地緣政治論文。普通營運敞口——在中國銷售、在深圳僱人——可以像德國公司描述在德國銷售那樣描述。你不能做的，是承諾一套你沒有的「全球」推薦名單，或找一個從未用過產品的美國推薦人，只為美中資本市場觀感去平衡投影片。

若沒有人願意說，就直說並解釋原因：階段太早、機密試點、受規管場景，或這星期不願碰政治相關問題的客戶。有理由的沉默，勝過會議結束後才慌忙找友善聲音，也勝過把 logo 放上投影片、指望沒人要電話。

這星期下次通話前的實務清單：

1. 點出具體的人，不只 logo。確認他們在既定時段會接聽。
2. 科學推薦與商業推薦分開。資料室不要混名單。
3. 就新聞氣氛向推薦人簡報，但不要寫政治講稿。他們應預期問題會被峰會與芯片新聞染色；不應被要求表演外交觀點。
4. 告訴投資者電話的界線。「他們談產品使用；不談出口許可」是乾淨的一句。
5. 任何經不起「星期四誰接電話？」的「客戶喜歡我們」，都該退役。

美中資本市場會繼續用 11 月 18 日電訊上的內容來讀中國故事。你的工作更窄：讓產品透過一個不是你的聲音變得可讀。峰會繁榮話改變房間的氣氛；它不改變對一個願意接電話的具名客戶的需要。

這是盡職審查紀律，不是投資建議，也不是斷言 APEC 或芯片政策會如何落定。它只提醒：當地緣政治改寫中國故事時，推薦電話仍是買家測試「故事是不是產品」的方式。
