::: en
The meeting you prepared for lasts forty-five minutes. You leave thinking it went well, because the questions were friendly and someone said the science was interesting. The meeting that decides anything happens after you have gone, with people who did not hear your tone of voice. They have a note, a deck, and ten minutes.

I care more about that second meeting than the first. You should write for it.

### Send the note they can forward

Within a day, send a short recap the person you met can paste to their partners. What you do, in two sentences. The one point they seemed to doubt, answered without a speech. What you are asking for. What you will send next, and on which day. No new adjectives. If your recap is longer than a page, it will be summarised by someone else, and you will not choose the summary.

Write it as if the partner who missed the meeting is the audience, because they are.

### Answer the question they did not want to ask in front of you

People are polite in the room and blunter in the corridor. If you sensed a worry — the structure, the single customer, the unfinished method — put the plain version in the recap, with the fact. Do not wait for them to formulate it badly in your absence. A worry you name and fence is smaller in the second meeting than a worry someone else invents from a vague sentence on page nine.

### One owner of the follow-up

Decide before you leave the building who on your side answers the next email. If three founders all reply, the fund sees a company that has not chosen a voice, and the thread forks. One voice, copying the others, is enough. The same rule you wanted inside your own data room applies here: a named person, who already knows where the contradiction is.

### Ask for the process, not for enthusiasm

"What happens on your side now, and when?" is a fair question at the end of a good meeting. The answer tells you whether there is an investment committee, a date, a partner who still needs to see you, or merely a person who enjoyed the conversation. Enjoyment is pleasant. It does not survive the second meeting unless someone is willing to spend political capital to put you on an agenda. If they will not tell you the process, assume there is not one yet, and do not count the meeting as a live round.

You did the performance. Then do the document that has to perform without you. That document is the one with a vote attached, whether or not anyone calls it that.

::: zh
你準備的那場會議持續四十五分鐘。你離開時覺得很好，因為問題友善，有人說科學很有趣。真正決定事情的會議在你走了之後，跟一些沒有聽到你語氣的人一起。他們有一份紀錄、一份簡報，和十分鐘。

我更在乎第二場。你應該為它而寫。

### 送出一份他們可以轉發的紀錄

一天之內，送一份短的回顧，讓見你的人可以貼給合夥人。你做甚麼，兩句。他們似乎懷疑的那一點，不用演講就答了。你要求甚麼。你下一步會送甚麼，哪一天。不要新的形容詞。如果你的回顧長過一頁，它會被別人摘要，而摘要不會由你選。

把它寫成缺席的那位合夥人才是讀者，因為他就是。

### 回答他們不願意當着你的面問的問題

人在房間裏禮貌，在走廊裏直接。如果你感覺到一個擔憂——架構、單一客戶、未完成的方法——把平實的版本和事實放進回顧。不要等他們在你不在時把它表述得很差。一個你點了名並圍起來的擔憂，在第二場會議裏，比一個別人從第九頁一句含糊的話發明出來的擔憂更小。

### 跟進要有一個負責人

離開大樓之前決定，下一封電郵由你們這邊誰回。如果三位創辦人都回，基金看到的是一家還沒有選好聲音的公司，而討論串會分叉。一個聲音，抄送其他人，就夠。你希望資料室裏有的那條規則，在這裏同樣適用：一個被點名的人，他已經知道矛盾在哪裏。

### 問流程，不要問熱情

「你們那邊現在會發生甚麼，何時？」在一場好的會議結束時是一個公平的問題。答案告訴你有沒有投資委員會、有沒有日期、有沒有一位仍需要見你的合夥人，或者只是一個人享受了這次對話。享受是愉快的。除非有人願意花政治資本把你放上議程，否則它活不過第二場會議。如果他們不願意告訴你流程，就假設目前沒有流程，不要把這次會議算成一輪活着的融資。

你做了表演。然後做那份必須在沒有你的情況下表演的文件。那份文件才是帶着一票的，無論有沒有人這樣叫它。
