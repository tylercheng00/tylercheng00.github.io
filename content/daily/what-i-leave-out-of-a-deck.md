Founders ask what to add before a meeting. I have a longer list of what to take out. Not because storytelling is a trick, and not because less information is always better. Because several kinds of page actively teach a careful reader to doubt the pages that were fine.

These are the ones I cut.

### The market slide that could belong to anyone

A trillion-dollar figure, three concentric circles, and a logo in the smallest circle. It says the prize is large and your aim is modest, which is the combination every company claims. Unless the number is built from customers you can name — units, price, how many of those buyers exist — the slide is borrowed confidence. Replace it with the first market you can actually reach, and the arithmetic of that market only. The big number can live in a sentence if you still love it. It should not be the third page.

### Adjectives standing in for evidence

"Leading", "proprietary", "unique", "AI-powered", "best-in-class". If you delete the adjective and the sentence collapses, the sentence was not carrying a fact. Say the fact. Granted patents, by number, and what they cover. A method that has a name. A result with a control. A team member who has done this exact job before. The reader will supply their own adjective if the fact earns it. They will discount yours.

### Six products when you have one

A roadmap that shows the whole future is comforting to draw and expensive to defend. Every extra product is a question you must now answer: who is doing it, what it costs, why it is not a distraction. If the honest answer is "not in this round", it does not belong in the main arc. One appendix page, clearly marked as later, is plenty. The body of the deck should be the company you are this year.

### Logos you have not earned

Partners who took a meeting. Accelerators that accepted you. Universities where someone once studied. A logo is a claim that a relationship exists of a certain kind. If you would be uneasy forwarding the slide to that organisation, take the logo off. Describe the relationship in words you would let them read. You will look smaller for one page and more reliable for the other eleven.

### A valuation before a reader has a company to value

Putting the proposed price on page four asks the reader to judge a number before they have agreed what the object is. Leave the ask to the end, or to the conversation, after the risk, the use of proceeds and the round's milestone are clear. A reader who has followed you that far can disagree with the price and still respect the file. A reader who meets the price first often never gets to the file.

Cut until every remaining page would still be worth printing if the meeting were only fifteen minutes. That is usually the meeting you are going to get.

創辦人在會議前問該加甚麼。我有一張更長的清單，是該拿掉甚麼。不是因為講故事是把戲，也不是因為資訊越少越好。是因為有幾種頁面，會主動教一個謹慎的讀者去懷疑那些本來沒問題的頁。

這些是我會剪掉的。

### 誰都可以用的市場頁

一個以萬億計的數字、三個同心圓，最小的圓裏有你的標誌。它說獎品很大、你的目標很謙虛，而這是每家公司都聲稱的組合。除非數字是從你叫得出名字的客戶建出來的——數量、價格、這樣的買家有多少——否則這一頁是借來的信心。把它換成你真正到得了的第一個市場，以及只屬於那個市場的算術。如果你仍然喜歡那個大數字，它可以活在一句話裏。它不該是第三頁。

### 代替證據的形容詞

「領先」、「專有」、「獨特」、「人工智能驅動」、「同級最佳」。如果你刪掉形容詞、句子就塌了，那句子本來就沒有載着事實。把事實說出來。已授權的專利，有數目，以及它們覆蓋甚麼。一個有名字的方法。一個有對照的結果。一個以前做過這一份工的團隊成員。如果事實值得，讀者會自己加上形容詞。他們會把你的形容詞打折。

### 你只有一個產品時的六個產品

一張畫出整個未來的路線圖，畫的時候令人安慰，辯護的時候很貴。每一個額外的產品都是你現在必須回答的問題：誰在做、要多少錢、為甚麼它不是分心。如果誠實的答案是「不在這一輪」，它就不屬於主線。附錄一頁，清楚標明是以後，就夠了。簡報的正文應該是你今年這家公司。

### 你還沒有贏得的標誌

只是見了面的夥伴。錄取了你的加速器。某人曾經讀過書的大學。一個標誌是在聲稱存在某種關係。如果你把這一頁轉發給那個機構會不安，就把標誌拿掉。用你願意讓他們讀到的文字描述關係。你會在一頁上看起來小一點，在其餘十一頁上更可靠。

### 讀者還沒有一家公司可以估值時的估值

在第四頁放上擬議的價格，是請讀者在同意那件東西是甚麼之前，先判斷一個數字。把要求留到最後，或留到對話裏，等風險、資金用途和這一輪的里程碑都清楚了。一個跟到那裏的讀者，可以不同意價格，仍然尊重這份文件。一個先遇到價格的讀者，常常再也走不到文件裏。

剪到每一頁剩下的，在會議只有十五分鐘時仍然值得印出來。那通常就是你會得到的會議。
