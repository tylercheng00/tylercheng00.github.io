::: en
Every listing document has a risk-factor section that could belong to almost any company in the industry. Competition. Key personnel. Regulation may change. These paragraphs are furniture. They are necessary, and almost nobody remembers them. Then there is the paragraph the team keeps rewriting. That one is the company.

I pay more attention to the argument around a risk factor than to the final adjectives.

### If it takes a week, it is doing its job

A risk factor that is easy to write is usually borrowed. A risk factor that produces four drafts and a tense call is touching something true: a customer who can leave, a permit that is not quite what the slide implied, a structure that works until a rule moves, a technology that has one step you do not yet control. Do not sand it down until a stranger could not tell it was yours. Specific is what makes a risk factor useful, to the reader and to you. It is also, inconveniently, what makes it quotable. That is the trade. Take it consciously.

### Write the fact, then the consequence

The weak version says the company "may be adversely affected". The useful version says what is true today, and what follows if it gets worse. Two customers are 70 percent of revenue, and if either leaves, the forecast you just showed does not survive. The patent is applied for, not granted, and the claim you need is the one the examiner has questioned. A reader can do something with that. A reader can do nothing with "adverse effects".

You do not need to dramatise. You need to be locatable. Someone who has your document open at 11pm should be able to point at the sentence and know what to ask in the morning.

### Do not hide it in the middle of a list

Teams bury the sharp paragraph between twelve dull ones, hoping mass will soften it. It does the opposite. A careful reader hunts for the paragraph that has proper nouns in it. Put the real risks where a person can find them, early in the section, in sentences a founder is willing to say out loud in a meeting. If you cannot say it out loud, you are not done arguing, and the document is ahead of the company.

### Use the rewrite as management

The week you spend on that paragraph is not a legal delay. It is information. Why is this sentence so hard? Because the fact is unresolved. Sometimes the right response is not better prose. It is to go and change the fact: a second customer, a signed assignment, a specification you are willing to stand behind. The document will then write itself in half a page. Until the fact moves, expect the paragraph to keep coming back, draft after draft, which is the document telling the truth ahead of schedule.

每一份上市文件都有一節風險因素，幾乎可以屬於行內任何一家公司。競爭。關鍵人員。法規可能改變。這些段落是家具。它們是必要的，幾乎沒有人記得。然後有一段是團隊不斷重寫的。那一段才是這家公司。

我更注意圍繞一項風險因素的爭論，多於最後的形容詞。

### 如果要一星期，它就在做它的工作

容易寫的風險因素，通常是借來的。引起四稿和一通緊張電話的風險因素，碰着了某件真的事：一個可以離開的客戶、一個跟投影片暗示的不完全一樣的許可、一個在規則挪動之前都有效的架構、一項還有一步你控制不了的技術。不要把它磨到陌生人看不出是你的。具體，才令風險因素有用，對讀者有用，對你也有用。不方便的是，它也因此可以被引用。這是交換。有意識地接受它。

### 先寫事實，再寫後果

弱的版本說公司「可能受到不利影響」。有用的版本說今天甚麼是真的，以及如果它變差，會跟着發生甚麼。兩個客戶佔收入七成，任何一個離開，你剛剛展示的預測就活不了。專利是已申請、未授權，而你需要的權利要求正是審查員質疑的那一項。讀者可以拿這個做點甚麼。讀者拿「不利影響」做不了任何事。

你不需要戲劇化。你需要可以被定位。一個人晚上十一時打開你的文件，應該可以指着那句話，知道早上要問甚麼。

### 不要把它藏在一張清單的中間

團隊把尖銳的那一段埋在十二段沉悶的中間，希望數量會把它軟化。效果相反。謹慎的讀者會去找那一段有專有名詞的。把真正的風險放在找得到的地方，放在這一節的前面，用創辦人願意在會議上大聲說出來的句子。如果你不能大聲說，你們就還沒爭論完，而文件已經跑在公司前面。

### 把重寫當成管理

你花在那一段上的一星期，不是法律上的延誤。它是資訊。為甚麼這句話這麼難？因為事實還沒有解決。有時正確的回應不是更好的文字。是去改變事實：第二個客戶、一份簽了的轉讓、一個你願意支撐的質量標準。然後文件會用半頁自己寫好。在事實挪動之前，預期這一段會一稿又一稿地回來。那是文件提前說了真話。
