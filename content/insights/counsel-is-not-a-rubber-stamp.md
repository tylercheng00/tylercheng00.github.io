::: en
On 18 November 2026 in Hong Kong time, the APEC CEO and business leaders gathering in Shenzhen is still the day’s noise. Speeches about Asia-Pacific prosperity and bay-area cooperation travel fast. So do shorter clips of leaders from Korea, Vietnam and Canada. Founders in the Greater Bay Area feel the atmosphere: doors open, rooms fill, someone says the week is a good time to send the deck.

Send the deck. Just do not treat counsel as a rubber stamp after the room has already nodded.

Summit weeks compress attention and expand loose language. A slide written for a Shenzhen breakfast can sound like policy commentary by the time it reaches a US counsel memo or a fund compliance check. “We sit at the centre of Asia-Pacific supply chains” is a fine dinner sentence. In diligence it becomes a question about where revenue, IP, data and control actually sit. Counsel’s job is not to kill the story. It is to make sure the story you tell under a flag-filled backdrop is the same story that survives a document request three weeks later.

Budget the review before you book the room. Founders often ask counsel to “quickly check” language that has already been shown — China exposure, related-party arrangements, AI or chip-adjacent partnerships, export-control footnotes that nod at Nvidia-era politics without naming a licence. That order is backwards. The paragraph you fear should meet counsel first. The paragraph you like can wait.

What counsel should see this week is not only the risk-factor appendix. Send the three slides you will speak from if someone asks about US–China capital markets after the summit clips. Send the one sentence on bay-area manufacturing if you claim it. Send the line that implies customer concentration in mainland China. Send anything that sounds like you are predicting policy from Xi’s Asia-Pacific prosperity themes. If counsel asks you to weaken a claim, that weaker claim is often the one you can defend when a buyer’s counsel asks for the contract behind the slide.

Export-control and AI-chip politics are still in the air this week — Jensen Huang, Nvidia, and the familiar argument about who may sell what where. You do not need a politics seminar in the deck. You do need counsel to read any sentence that sounds like you have cleared a control, secured a GPU allocation, or built a “US-safe” story on a handshake. Marketing language that dies under a document request costs more than a dull accurate line. Dull accurate lines close more often than summit slogans.

A clean deck that counsel has not seen is not clean. It is untested. Under summit-week scrutiny, “untested” travels farther. A partner who liked your energy in Shenzhen may forward the PDF to New York overnight. New York will not remember the banquet. It will remember whether your China-exposure paragraph matches the corporate chart.

Practical sequence for founders raising into US–China capital markets this week:

1. Freeze the risky paragraphs — ownership, IP, related parties, data location, any AI/compute dependency — and send them to counsel with the corporate diagram.
2. Ask for redlines with a clock: what must change before the next meeting, what can wait for the long-form memo.
3. Update the spoken script to match the redlines. Do not keep a stronger oral version and a weaker written one.
4. Only then put the deck in front of buyers who are watching the same summit headlines you are.

Counsel is not there to bless the atmosphere. Counsel is there so that when the atmosphere fades — and summit weeks always fade — the sentences still stand. If you only send the paragraphs you like, you have hired a rubber stamp. If you send the paragraphs you fear, you have hired a lawyer.

This is operational hygiene for a volatile news week. It is not investment advice, and it is not a prediction about APEC outcomes. It is a reminder that prosperity speeches and diligence checklists run on different clocks — and only one of those clocks belongs to your counsel.

以香港時間計，2026 年 11 月 18 日，深圳舉行的 APEC 工商領導人峰會仍是當日焦點。亞太繁榮、灣區合作的演講傳得很快，韓、越、加等領導人的短片亦然。大灣區創辦人會感到氣氛轉熱：門打開、會議室坐滿，有人說這星期適合把簡報寄出去。

可以寄。但不要等房間已經點頭，才把律師當成事後蓋章的橡皮圖章。

峰會期間，注意力被壓縮，用語卻容易變鬆。為深圳早餐會寫的一頁，到了美國律師備忘錄或基金合規審查，聽起來可能像政策評論。「我們位處亞太供應鏈中心」晚宴上很好聽；盡職審查時，卻變成收入、知識產權、數據與控制權實際落在何處的問題。律師的工作不是抹殺故事，而是確保你在旗海背景下講的故事，三星期後面對文件索取時仍然站得住。

審閱要排在訂會議室之前。創辦人常請律師「快速過目」已經展示過的句子——中國風險敞口、關聯交易、與 AI 或芯片相關的合作，以及點到 Nvidia 時代政治卻不點出許可證的出口管制腳註。次序反了。你最怕被追問的段落應先交律師；你喜歡的段落可以稍後再看。

這星期律師該看的，不只是風險因素附錄。峰會短片過後，若有人問起美中資本市場，你打算照着講的那三頁，要先寄出去。若你主張灣區製造，那一句也要寄。暗示內地客戶過度集中的那一行，要寄。任何聽起來像藉習近平亞太繁榮論調預測政策的句子，同樣要寄。若律師要求把說法寫弱，那較克制的版本，往往才是買家律師追問投影片背後合約時守得住的說法。

出口管制與 AI 芯片政治這星期仍未散去——黃仁勳、Nvidia，以及誰可在何處出售甚麼的舊爭論。簡報不需要變成政治研討會；但任何聽起來像已過關管制、已鎖定 GPU 配額，或僅憑握手就建成「對美安全」故事的句子，都必須經律師過目。經不起文件索取的華麗行銷，代價高過一句平淡但準確的話。平淡準確的句子，成交機會往往高過峰會口號。

律師沒看過的「乾淨」簡報並不乾淨，只是未經檢驗。峰會期間審視加嚴，「未經檢驗」傳得更遠。在深圳欣賞你氣場的合夥人，可能連夜把 PDF 轉到紐約。紐約不會記得宴會，只會核對你的中國風險段落是否與公司架構圖一致。

這星期面向美中資本市場融資的創辦人，可按以下次序處理：

1. 先鎖定高風險段落——權屬、知識產權、關聯方、數據存放地、任何 AI／算力依賴——連同架構圖一併寄給律師。
2. 要求附時限的紅線修改：下次會議前必須改甚麼，甚麼可留待長文備忘錄。
3. 口頭講稿與紅線對齊。不要保留較強的口頭版與較弱的書面版。
4. 然後才把簡報交到同樣在看峰會新聞的買家面前。

律師不是來為氣氛背書的。律師是為了氣氛退去之後——峰會熱潮總會退去——句子仍然站得住。若你只寄喜歡的段落，雇的是橡皮圖章；若你寄最怕被追問的段落，雇的才是律師。

這是新聞波動週的營運紀律，不是投資建議，也不是對 APEC 結果的預測。它只提醒：繁榮演講與盡職審查清單，走在不同的時鐘上——而其中一座，屬於你的律師。
