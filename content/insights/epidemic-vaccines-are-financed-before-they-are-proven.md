::: en
On Friday, 27 November 2026, the Uganda Virus Research Institute announced that it had launched Africa’s first mRNA clinical trial of an investigational vaccine against Bundibugyo ebolavirus, working with Moderna. The study, known as mRNA-1469-P102, is a Phase 1 trial and will enrol about 224 healthy volunteers aged 1 to 65. It is the first test of this vaccine in children from one year of age, and volunteers will be followed for about a year. A first trial, in adults aged 18 to 65, began in Canada in August. According to UVRI, interim results from that trial contributed to a recommendation by a WHO advisory group that the vaccine be tested in a Phase 2/3 ring-vaccination trial in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The Ugandan work is supported by funding of up to US$58.5 million from the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations, or CEPI, to Moderna for its Bundibugyo programme.

The disease backdrop is severe. Congolese government data reported on Thursday showed at least 12,021 confirmed infections and 5,904 deaths since the outbreak began in mid-May, according to Al Jazeera. AFP, citing the WHO, reported that there are currently no licensed vaccines or treatments for the Bundibugyo strain, and that two experimental vaccines are due to begin testing in the Congo on Monday.

The second vaccine is the Oxford–Serum Institute candidate, ChAdOx1 BDBV, and the way it is being financed is the more instructive detail. On 24 November Gavi announced an advance purchase commitment with the Serum Institute of India for up to 1.5 million doses. It is funded through Gavi’s First Response Fund, and Gavi said the doses would be procured only if clinical trials succeed and regulatory approvals and policy recommendations follow. Gavi said the Serum Institute has already manufactured drug substance for more than half a million doses so that supply can start quickly if the results are positive. Up to US$40 million from the fund has been set aside for vaccine access deals of this kind. A Gavi official told AFP the organisation is investing in the hope that the signals will be positive, and that if they are, it will have gained months of deployment time.

Put the two pieces together and a pattern appears that biotech investors rarely see in ordinary markets. In a normal commercial programme, the sponsor funds the trial, the regulator decides, and a buyer appears afterwards. In an epidemic, the sequence is compressed and reordered. A public-private funder pays for development before there is efficacy data. A purchaser commits to demand before there is approval. A manufacturer builds stock before there is a licence. Each party takes a piece of the risk that, in a commercial setting, would sit entirely with the developer’s shareholders.

What can a private company learn from this without pretending it is in the same position?

First, separate the kinds of money. Non-dilutive grants and milestone-based funding have different conditions from equity. They often carry deliverables, data-sharing expectations and access terms that shape the later commercial plan. Read those terms as part of the business model, not as a favour.

Second, understand conditional demand. An advance purchase commitment is valuable because it signals a buyer, but its own terms say the order depends on trial results, approvals and policy recommendations. It lowers the risk of manufacturing at scale. It does not remove the clinical risk, and the company should not describe it as revenue.

Third, plan at-risk manufacturing deliberately. Building stock before approval is a financing decision as much as an operational one. Decide how many doses can be written off if the trial fails, and who pays for that outcome.

Fourth, keep the timeline honest. A Gavi official told AFP that trials could conclude in the first half of 2027, or in the first quarter under a more optimistic scenario. Those are hopes with ranges, not commitments, and they should be treated that way in any plan.

This note takes no view on Moderna, the Serum Institute or any listed company, and it is not a forecast of the outbreak. It is a research-desk reading of how risk is shared when the usual market is missing. It is not investment advice.

11月27日（星期五），烏干達病毒研究所宣布，與Moderna合作，在當地啟動非洲首個針對本迪布焦埃博拉病毒的mRNA疫苗臨床試驗。這項編號mRNA-1469-P102的一期試驗，預計招募約224名一至65歲的健康志願者，也是這款疫苗首次在一歲以上兒童身上測試，志願者會被追蹤約一年。第一項試驗8月在加拿大展開，對象是18至65歲成人。據研究所說明，該試驗的中期結果，促成世界衞生組織的一個諮詢小組建議，把這款疫苗拿到剛果民主共和國，進行二／三期環形接種試驗。烏干達這項研究獲得流行病防範創新聯盟（CEPI）資助，Moderna這項本迪布焦疫苗計劃最多可獲5,850萬美元。

疫情本身相當嚴峻。據半島電視台引述剛果政府星期四的數據，自5月中疫情爆發以來，至少有12,021宗確診，5,904人死亡。法新社引述世衞說，目前針對本迪布焦病毒株，沒有任何已獲許可的疫苗或治療方法；兩款試驗性疫苗將於星期一開始在剛果境內測試。

第二款疫苗是牛津大學與印度血清研究所的ChAdOx1 BDBV，而它的融資方式，其實是更值得細看的地方。11月24日，Gavi宣布與印度血清研究所簽訂預先採購承諾，最多購買150萬劑。這筆採購由Gavi的「首輪應對基金」支付，而且Gavi說明，只有在臨床試驗成功、其後取得監管批准和政策建議之後，才會真正採購。Gavi指出，血清研究所已經生產了足夠超過50萬劑的原料藥，一旦結果理想，就可以盡快供貨。基金中最多4,000萬美元，已經劃撥給這類疫苗取得途徑的協議。Gavi一位官員對法新社說，機構是在期待結果向好的前提下投入，若結果真的理想，部署時間可以提前好幾個月。

把兩件事放在一起看，會見到一個在一般生技市場中很少出現的格局。在正常的商業開發中，是由研發公司出錢做試驗，監管機構作決定，買家在批准之後才出現。疫情之中，次序被壓縮、被重新排列：公共與私營的資助者，在還沒有療效數據之前就出錢支持研發；買家在還沒獲批之前，就先承諾需求；生產商在還沒有牌照之前，就先備貨。每一方都分擔了一部分風險，而這些風險在商業環境裏，本來會全部落在研發公司股東的身上。

一間私人公司可以從中學到甚麼，同時又不必假裝自己處於同樣的處境？

第一，把不同性質的資金分開來看。不涉及股權攤薄的資助與按里程碑發放的撥款，條件跟股權融資不同，往往附帶交付成果、數據共享的期望，以及日後影響商業計劃的取得途徑條款。這些條款要當成商業模式的一部分來讀，不是人情。

第二，弄清楚附帶條件的需求。預先採購承諾之所以有價值，是因為它顯示有買家；但承諾本身的條款也說明，訂單取決於試驗結果、批准和政策建議。它降低了大規模生產的風險，卻沒有消除臨床風險，公司也不應該把它說成收入。

第三，有意識地規劃「先生產、後批准」。在批准之前備貨，既是營運決定，也是融資決定。要事先決定：如果試驗失敗，可以接受註銷多少劑量，這個損失又由誰承擔。

第四，時間表要實事求是。Gavi官員對法新社說，試驗可能在2027年上半年完成，樂觀的話是第一季。這些都是帶着區間的期望，而不是承諾，在任何計劃裏都應該這樣處理。

本文對Moderna、印度血清研究所或任何上市公司都沒有立場，也不是對疫情走勢的預測，只是研究桌面對一個問題的觀察：在通常的市場不存在時，風險是怎樣分擔的。本文不構成投資建議。
