::: en
This week from Hong Kong, the screens are full of leaders in Shenzhen for APEC, trade and investment talk, and a rolling story about Trump, Xi and Putin sharing airtime. Separately, Jensen Huang and Nvidia sit under Senate banking scrutiny, with a bipartisan push to block more AI chip sales into China, while the White House plays down any Nvidia “tragedy.” That mix moves risk appetite for China-linked names and for anyone raising into a US listing path. It does not rewrite how a convertible bridge should be built.

Bridges are tools. They are not strategies. Before you sign, write what the bridge is for: which milestone, which process, which date you expect to reopen the round. If the only answer is we need more time because the summit week is noisy, you are buying calendar without a destination. Macro fog is a reason to check assumptions. It is not a substitute for an endgame.

Summit-week uncertainty shows up in three places founders often leave blank. First, the next process: priced round, strategic close, or filing window. Second, the gate that must be true before that process starts — a cleaned data room, a paid customer, a finished study, counsel clearance on a China-exposure page. Third, the date you will either reopen or admit the plan failed. Without those three, the note becomes a habit: every soft week produces another short cheque and another reset of the same deck.

Investors who provide bridges will ask what changed since the last raise. Have that sentence ready in language that survives a partner meeting where someone just read chip-export headlines. Cap table cleanup, a paid pilot, a method that transferred to a second lab — something that makes the next conversation different. A bridge that only funds the same slides again trains everyone to expect another bridge. In a week when US–China–Russia angles dominate the feed, “we are waiting for clarity from Shenzhen” is not a change. Clarity for your company is something you produce on paper.

Price the optionality honestly. A bridge that converts at a discount into a round that may slip three months is not free money. Write the discount, the cap, the interest, and what happens if the endgame date arrives with no close. Who gets a seat at the next board. Whether the note blocks a cleaner lead. Whether you can still take a strategic cheque without a fight. Those clauses matter more when capital markets are pricing China listing and financing risk off diplomacy headlines rather than off your last customer call.

Build a one-page bridge memo before the lawyers circulate drafts. Top third: use of proceeds in weeks, not themes. Middle: the milestone that must be hit, with a owner and a date. Bottom: the reopen path — who you will call, with what package, and what “enough” looks like for a yes. Attach the China-operating map if US readers will see the deal: where cash sits, where IP sits, which permissions the operating company needs. The memo is not theatre. It is how you stop a polite bridge from becoming a series of smaller emergencies.

Watch the calendar against the news cycle. APEC leaders week ends; chip-sale politics will not. Do not set your reopen date for the day after a photo opportunity and then act surprised when banking diligence still asks about export controls, related parties, and manufacturing sites. Set the date against your own gates. If those gates need six weeks of lab and counsel work, match the bridge to that work, not to a summit.

Founders sometimes treat a bridge as proof the company is still alive. Alive is not the standard. The standard is whether the next close has a shape. If you cannot draw that shape this morning in Hong Kong, while the summit coverage is loudest, wait one more day before you sign and write the shape. Expensive silence is what you get when the money arrives and the sentence about the endgame never does.

If you already signed without an endgame, write one now and send it to the note holders. Tell them the milestone, the date, and what you will stop doing if the date slips. Investors dislike surprises more than hard news. A bridge with a named endgame can still fail. A bridge with no endgame fails quietly for months, then loudly when someone asks why the calendar only ever moved sideways.

這個星期從香港看螢幕，深圳 APEC 領袖週、貿易與投資會談，以及特朗普、習近平、普京同框的報道佔滿版面。另一邊，黃仁勳與英偉達正面對參議院銀行委員會的審視，兩黨聯手推動進一步限制對華人工智能芯片出口，白宮則淡化所謂英偉達「悲劇」。這一套會動對中國相關名字的風險偏好，也會動任何走美國上市路徑的融資氣氛。它不會改寫可換股橋接該怎麼建。

橋接是工具，不是策略。簽名之前，寫下這筆橋為了甚麼：哪個里程碑、哪段流程、你預期何時重開這輪。若唯一答案是峰會週太吵、我們需要更多時間，你是在買日曆，沒有目的地。宏觀的霧是檢查假設的理由，不是結局的替代品。

峰會週的不確定，常落在創辦人空白的三處。第一，下一程流程：定價輪、戰略交割，或申報窗口。第二，流程開始前必須為真的關口——清理過的資料室、付費客戶、做完的研究、律師過目的中國風險一頁。第三，你會重開、或承認計劃失敗的日期。沒有這三樣，票據會變成習慣：每個軟的星期再來一張短支票，同一份簡報再重置一次。

提供橋接的投資者會問：距上次融資改變了甚麼。準備好那句話，而且要能活過合夥人剛讀完芯片出口標題的會議。股權表清理、付費試點、轉到第二個實驗室的方法——任何讓下一次對話不同的東西。只為同一份投影片再供一次電的橋，會訓練所有人期待下一座橋。在美中俄角度霸佔信息流的一週，「我們在等深圳帶來的清晰」不算改變。對你公司而言的清晰，是你在紙上做出來的。

把選擇權定價說清楚。以折扣轉入一輪可能再拖三個月的橋，不是免費的錢。寫折扣、上限、利息，以及結局日到了卻沒有交割時會怎樣。下一屆董事會誰有席位。這張票據會否擋掉更乾淨的領投。你還能不能在不打架的情況下接戰略支票。當資本市場按外交標題替中國公司上市與融資定價、而不是按你上次客戶電話定價時，這些條款更重要。

律師傳草稿之前，先做一頁橋接備忘。上三分一：資金用途按星期寫，不要主題口號。中間：必須打中的里程碑，有負責人、有日期。下三分一：重開路徑——你會打給誰、帶甚麼材料包、「夠」到可以說 yes 長甚麼樣子。若美國讀者會看到這筆交易，附上中國營運圖：現金在哪、知識產權在哪、營運公司要哪些許可。這頁不是表演。它是阻止一筆客氣的橋變成一連串小緊急的方法。

把日曆對着新聞周期看。APEC 領袖週會結束；芯片銷售政治不會。不要把重開日設在合照翌日，然後對銀行盡職審查仍在問出口管制、關聯方與製造場地感到意外。日期應對着你自己的關口。若關口需要六個星期的實驗室與律師工作，橋就配那份工作，不要配一場峰會。

創辦人有時把橋接當成公司還活着的證明。活着不是標準。標準是下一次交割有沒有形狀。若你今早在香港、峰會報道最響的時候仍畫不出那個形狀，簽名前再等一天，先把形狀寫出來。錢到了、結局的那句話卻從未出現，你得到的就是昂貴的沉默。

若你已經在沒有結局的情況下簽了，現在就寫，並送給票據持有人。告訴他們里程碑、日期，以及日期滑了你會停止做甚麼。投資者不喜歡驚喜，甚於不喜歡硬消息。有具名結局的橋仍可能失敗。沒有結局的橋會安靜地失敗好幾個月，然後有人問：為甚麼日曆永遠只橫着移。
