The data room is the pitch資料室本身就是簡報

Investors decide how serious you are from the folder you send, before they decide what they think of the story.投資者在判斷故事之前，先從你交出的資料夾判斷你有多認真。

US capital markets美國資本市場8 Nov 20262026年11月8日4 min read閱讀約 4 分鐘

Founders spend weeks on the deck. Then an investor asks for the data room, and what comes back is a zip file named final_v7, with half the documents missing and three versions of the cap table.

I have come to think the folder is the pitch. The slides are the introduction. The room is where someone decides whether to spend another meeting on you.

What belongs there on day one

Not everything. The right things, named so a stranger can find them.

  • A cap table that matches the registers, including options and any convertible instruments
  • The last two years of financials, even if they are management accounts, clearly marked as such
  • The contracts that actually matter: the largest customers, the key suppliers, the IP assignments
  • A one-page chart of who owns the operating company

If one of those is still being updated, say so in a note at the top. Silence reads as avoidance. A short, honest gap is easier to live with than a file an investor later finds out was incomplete.

The file name is part of the work

"Contract.pdf" and "Contract (2).pdf" waste a lawyer's afternoon. Date the file, name the counterparty, and put superseded versions in a folder called old. This sounds trivial. It is the difference between a question answered the same day and a question that waits until someone reconstructs what you meant.

I look at the folder structure before I look at the model. A company that cannot label its own contracts will struggle with every later request, and there will be many.

One owner

Someone inside the company has to own the room. Not the advisor, and not "the team". When a number in the deck disagrees with a number in the financials, that person should already know. They will be asked. The pause before the answer is part of the answer.

Advisors can set the index up. They should not be the only people who know where the employment agreements are. On the week a deal is trying to close, the advisor is in other meetings. The company still has to answer.

What the room says when you are not there

Most of the reading happens without you. There is no chance to explain the footnote. A tidy room says the company can be diligenced. A messy one says every later step will feel like this. Investors price that, whether they say so or not.

Build the room before you need it. The week you are trying to close is the worst week to assemble it. By then you are negotiating, and negotiation is a bad time to discover that last year's option grants were never written down.

創辦人花幾個星期打磨簡報。然後投資者要資料室，收回來的是一個名叫 final_v7 的壓縮檔：缺了一半文件，股權表有三個版本。

我後來把那個資料夾看成簡報本身。投影片只是開場。別人決定要不要再花一次會，是在資料室裏決定的。

第一天就該放好的東西

不是全部，是對的那幾樣，而且外人找得到。

  • 一張跟登記冊吻合的股權表，包括購股權和所有可換股工具
  • 近兩年的財務，即使只是管理賬，也要標明
  • 真正要緊的合約：最大的客戶、關鍵供應商、知識產權轉讓
  • 一頁圖，寫明誰擁有營運主體

如果其中一項仍在更新，在首頁用一句話說明。不說，人家會當成你在閃避。一個短而誠實的缺口，好過投資者後來發現檔案根本不完整。

檔名也是工作的一部分

「合約.pdf」和「合約 (2).pdf」會浪費律師一個下午。檔名寫上日期和對方是誰，舊版本放進一個叫 old 的資料夾。聽起來很瑣碎。分別在於：問題是當天有答案，還是要等人重新拼出你的意思。

我會先看資料夾的結構，才看財務模型。一家公司如果連自己的合約都標不清楚，後面每一個要求都會吃力，而要求一定很多。

要有一個負責人

資料室要由公司裏的一個人負責，不是顧問，也不是「團隊」。簡報上的數字和財務裏的數字對不上時，這個人應該早就知道。因為一定會被問，而回答前的停頓，本身就是答案的一部分。

顧問可以幫忙建索引。他們不該是唯一知道僱傭合約放在哪裏的人。成交的那一週，顧問在別的會上。公司仍然要答。

你不在場時，資料室仍在說話

大部分閱讀發生在你不在的時候，沒有機會解釋腳註。整齊的資料室表示這家公司承受得起盡職審查。混亂的資料室表示後面每一步都會是這種感覺。投資者會為這件事定價，即使他們不說出來。

在需要之前就把資料室建好。打算成交的那一週，是最不該才開始整理的一週。那時你在談判，而談判時才發現去年的購股權從未寫下來，是最壞的時機。
