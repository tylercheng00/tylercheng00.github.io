::: en
A meeting about a US listing or a financing has too many professionals in it. Everyone is competent. Everyone wants to be useful. Within ten minutes, the lawyer is answering a commercial question, the founder is guessing at an accounting treatment, and the advisor is making a promise the company has not agreed to. Nothing on fire. Everything slightly worse.

The fix is dull, and it works. Before you walk in, decide who owns which sentence.

### The company owns the facts

What you sell, to whom, on what terms. What happened last year. What the money is for. What you will not do. These sentences should come from a founder or the finance lead, in their own words. If they cannot, the meeting is early, and more advisors will not make it late enough. An advisor can rehearse. An advisor should not perform the company's memory.

### Counsel owns the document, not the deal

Lawyers should speak when the question is what the paper says, what it does not say, and what it would take to change it. They should not be the people who decide, out loud, whether a valuation is acceptable or whether a customer is strategic. When they are pulled into that, pull them back, politely, in the room. Investors notice who is allowed to bind the company. If the answer appears to be "whoever spoke last", the next draft gets more cautious, not less.

### The auditor owns the numbers they will sign

If a figure will be audited, the person who will sign the opinion is the person who should describe its limits. Not the banker, and not a slide. "The revenue is recognised when..." is an auditor's sentence when the policy is still being argued. Let them say the unfinished part. A smooth number delivered by someone who will not sign it is how meetings end with false comfort.

### The advisor owns the process

Timing, who has been seen, what the other side asked, what the next document is, what is blocked. That is the advisor's patch. The moment an advisor starts inventing the company's science, or its five-year market, they are spending credibility they do not have. I would rather an advisor say "that one is for the founder" than fill a silence with a plausible paragraph. Silences can be survived. A plausible paragraph that the founder then contradicts cannot.

### One person chairs

Someone has to be allowed to interrupt. Usually the founder, sometimes the advisor if the founder asks them to run the clock. Their job is not to talk the most. It is to hand the next question to the right mouth, and to stop a small factual dispute from eating the half hour you needed for the actual decision.

Write the roles in four lines and read them in the taxi. It feels formal. It is cheaper than repairing a meeting in which four people answered one question four ways.

::: zh
一場關於赴美上市或融資的會議，專業人士太多。每個人都能幹。每個人都想有用。十分鐘之內，律師在答一個商業問題，創辦人在猜一個會計處理，顧問在做一個公司沒有同意的承諾。沒有着火。每一件事都稍微變差。

修理的方法很悶，而且有效。走進房間之前，決定哪一句話屬於誰。

### 公司擁有事實

你賣甚麼、賣給誰、甚麼條款。去年發生了甚麼。錢用來做甚麼。你不會做甚麼。這些句子應該由創辦人或財務負責人，用自己的話說出來。如果他們不能，會議就太早，更多顧問不會把它變得夠晚。顧問可以排練。顧問不該表演公司的記憶。

### 律師擁有文件，不擁有交易

當問題是文件寫了甚麼、沒寫甚麼、要改它需要甚麼，律師應該說話。他們不該是當眾決定估值可不可接受、或一個客戶是不是策略性的人。當他們被拉進去，在房間裏禮貌地把他們拉回來。投資者注意到誰可以拘束這家公司。如果答案看起來是「最後說話的那位」，下一稿會更謹慎，而不是更放鬆。

### 核數師擁有他們將要簽署的數字

如果一個數字會被審計，描述它的界限的人，應該是將要簽署意見的人。不是銀行家，也不是一頁投影片。當政策仍在爭論，「收入在……時確認」是核數師的句子。讓他們說未完成的部分。一個不會簽字的人送出的光滑數字，是會議以虛假的安慰結束的方式。

### 顧問擁有流程

時間、見過誰、對方問了甚麼、下一份文件是甚麼、卡在哪裏。這是顧問的範圍。一旦顧問開始發明公司的科學，或它的五年市場，他們是在花自己沒有的信用。我寧願顧問說「這句屬於創辦人」，而不是用一段聽起來合理的話填補沉默。沉默可以活下來。一段合理、然後被創辦人否定的話，不能。

### 要有一個人主持

必須有人被允許打斷。通常是創辦人，有時是顧問，如果創辦人請他們看時間。他們的工作不是說得最多。是把下一個問題交給正確的嘴，並阻止一個小的事實爭議吃掉你需要用來做真正決定的那半小時。

把角色寫成四行，在的士上讀一次。感覺很正式。它比修補一場四個人用四種方式回答同一個問題的會議便宜。
