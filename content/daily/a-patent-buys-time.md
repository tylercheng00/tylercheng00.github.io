::: en
A patent slide often arrives early in a biotech or deep-tech deck, with a number and a map of the world. The feeling it is meant to create is safety: we own this. Sometimes that will be true, years from now, in a narrower way than the map suggests. On the day you are raising, a filed application is usually a bet you have paid to keep open.

I read a patent slide as a clock and a claim set. I do not read it as revenue.

### What is filed, what is granted, what is yours

Three different facts get collapsed into "we have IP". An application is a request. A granted patent is a right, in one country, with claims that survived an examiner, which may be narrower than what you wanted. Ownership is a chain of assignments from every person who contributed, including students, contractors and the collaborator who "was just helping". If any link is missing, the right sits in the wrong place, and a number of filings does not repair it.

The slide I trust has those three facts in separate lines. Count of applications. Count of grants, and where. A sentence that every inventor has assigned to the company that is raising. If the third sentence is not true, it is the first thing to fix, ahead of any new filing.

### Claims are the product, not the certificate

Investors who are not patent lawyers will not read the document. You still need to be able to say, in ordinary words, what a competitor is not allowed to do if the claims you care about are granted. If you cannot say that without pointing at a diagram of a molecule and hoping, the claims are not yet a business fact. They are a scientific description. Descriptions are useful. They are not the same as a fence.

Ask your counsel for that sentence in writing. If they will not give it without a page of caveats, put the caveats in the appendix and keep the sentence honest. "We hope to cover the method of making it" is a fine sentence. "Protected worldwide" is not, unless you are ready to point at the grants.

### The bill is part of the strategy

Filing in many countries feels like ambition. Each one is a future invoice: examination, translations, annuities, and the local counsel who answers them. A young company can file itself into a fixed cost it cannot carry, then abandon families just as they become interesting, which is the worst time to abandon them. Choose the countries the way you choose a first market. Where a competitor would actually make or sell. Where you can pay for the next five years without pretending the round after this one is guaranteed.

### Time is the thing you bought

A filing date holds your place against later filers. That is real, and it is worth paying for when the idea is ready to be described. It does not stop a well-funded team from working around you, and it does not make a customer buy. Use the time to get the assignment chain clean, the claim sentence sharp, and the product real enough that the patent is attached to something a buyer can recognise. A fence around an empty field is still a fence. It is not a business.

專利那一頁常常在生物科技或深科技的簡報裏很早出現，帶着一個數字和一張世界地圖。它想製造的感覺是安全：這是我們的。有時多年以後這會是真的，而且比地圖所暗示的更窄。在你融資的這一天，一份已提交的申請通常是一個你付了錢、讓它繼續開着的賭注。

我把專利頁讀成一個時鐘和一組權利要求。我不把它讀成收入。

### 申請了甚麼，授權了甚麼，是誰的

三件不同的事被壓進「我們有知識產權」。申請是一個請求。授權的專利是一項權利，在一個國家，權利要求活過了審查員，而那可能比你想要的更窄。擁有權是一條從每一位貢獻者轉讓過來的鏈，包括學生、承包商，和那位「只是幫忙」的合作者。任何一環缺失，權利就坐在錯誤的地方，而申請的數目補不了它。

我信任的那一頁，把這三件事實分成三行。申請的數目。授權的數目，以及在哪裏。一句話：每一位發明人都已經轉讓給這家正在融資的公司。如果第三句不是真的，它是第一件要修的事，優先於任何新的申請。

### 權利要求才是產品，不是證書

不是專利律師的投資者不會讀那份文件。你仍然要能夠用普通的話說：如果你在乎的那些權利要求被授權，競爭對手不可以做甚麼。如果你不指着一張分子圖、然後希望對方明白，就說不出這句話，權利要求就還不是一個商業事實。它們是一段科學描述。描述是有用的。它們不是圍欄。

請你的律師把那句話寫下來。如果他們不附一頁保留就不能給，把保留放進附錄，讓句子保持誠實。「我們希望覆蓋它的製造方法」是一句好句子。「全球受保護」不是，除非你準備好指出那些授權。

### 賬單是策略的一部分

在很多國家申請，感覺像野心。每一個都是將來的發票：審查、翻譯、年費，以及回答它們的當地律師。一家年輕的公司可以把自己申請到一個負擔不起的固定成本，然後在專利族變得有趣的時候放棄它們，那是最不該放棄的時候。選國家的方式，應該像選第一個市場。競爭對手真正會生產或銷售的地方。未來五年你付得起、而不必假裝下一輪是保證了的地方。

### 你買到的是時間

申請日讓你在後來的申請人面前佔着位置。這是真的，當這個想法已經準備好被描述，它值得付。它阻止不了一個有資金的團隊繞過你，它也不會讓客戶購買。用這段時間把轉讓鏈弄乾淨、把權利要求那句話磨尖、把產品做得真實到專利是附着在買家認得出的東西上。空地周圍的圍欄仍然是圍欄。它不是一門生意。
