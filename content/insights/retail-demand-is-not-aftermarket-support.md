On 24 November 2026 Beijing Sinotau International Pharmaceutical Technology began trading on the Main Board of the Hong Kong exchange under stock code 9941. Law-firm and trade-press accounts describe two firsts. It is the first radiopharmaceutical company to list under Chapter 18A, the exchange’s route for biotechnology companies that do not yet meet the profit or revenue tests, and it is the first initial public offering under the Uncertificated Securities Market regime that went live on 16 November. The offering raised about HK$341 million before any over-allotment option. Trade press reported that the shares opened below the offer price on the first day despite strong demand from retail investors. Read from Hong Kong on 27 November, the case offers a clear lesson for founders who are thinking about an 18A listing: oversubscription and aftermarket trading are two separate questions.

Start with the structure. In a Hong Kong offering the public tranche is open to retail applicants for several days, and demand in that tranche is reported as a subscription multiple. A high multiple shows that many people applied. It does not show how many of them intend to hold, how many will sell on the first morning, or whether institutional buyers who took the larger international tranche plan to add or reduce. The first day’s price reflects the balance of those decisions in a few hours, and the balance can differ from the balance of demand in the bookbuilding phase.

Then consider why a new category matters. The company’s prospectus describes a pipeline of diagnostic and therapeutic radiopharmaceuticals, including products at the registrational stage, and states that one core product is expected to receive approval in mainland China in December 2026. Investors who have not seen a listed peer must decide what multiple to apply and which comparables to use. A first-of-its-kind listing gives them no peer group, so the price-discovery process is more exposed to the mood of the day. For a founder, the practical point is that being first in a category is not automatically a pricing advantage.

The new paperless regime is a further point of interest, although its effect on trading is not yet measurable from a single debut. Under an uncertificated system, share records are held electronically and settlement steps change for brokers, registrars and investors. Any operational friction in the first weeks is a matter for the intermediaries rather than the issuer, but an issuer should still know how its own register will work, who the contacts are and what happens if a holder queries a record.

What would a careful 18A team take from this? First, plan the investor mix, not just the raise. Ask your sponsors how many cornerstone and anchor investors they expect, what lock-up terms apply and how the allocation between retail and institutional holders was set. A book that is heavily weighted to short-term retail demand can look excellent on the day applications close and be less steady a week later.

Second, prepare the stabilisation conversation early. Hong Kong offerings commonly include an over-allotment arrangement and a stabilising manager, and the rules on what that manager may do are defined. Founders should read the relevant section of their own prospectus and understand how it would operate in practice, rather than assume it will cover a weak opening.

Third, write the first-year disclosure calendar before the listing, not after. List the dates for annual results, interim results, clinical readouts, regulatory decisions and lock-up expiries. A listed company is judged on how its news arrives as much as on what the news says.

A listing-day opening is one observation, and one observation says very little about a business. Sinotau’s later trading, its progress on registrational products and its financial results will say more than its first morning. For founders considering a Hong Kong raise, the more durable lesson is procedural: a strong book, a sound prospectus and a plan for the weeks after listing are three different deliverables. This note is a research-desk view, not investment advice and not a comment on the merits of any listed company.

2026年11月24日，北京先通國際醫藥科技（股份代號9941）在香港交易所主板開始買賣。律師行與財經媒體的報道都點出兩個「首宗」：它是首間以第18A章上市的放射性藥物公司——18A是為尚未達到盈利或收入要求的生物科技公司而設的上市途徑；同時也是11月16日啟動的「無紙證券市場」新制度下的首宗首次公開招股。這次發售在未計超額配股權前，集資約3.41億港元。財經媒體報道，儘管散戶認購踴躍，股份首日仍以低於發行價開市。從香港11月27日回頭看，這宗個案給有意走18A路線的創辦人一個很清楚的提醒：超額認購與上市後的買賣，是兩個不同的問題。

先看結構。香港的新股發售，公開發售部分會向散戶開放數日，這部分的需求通常以超額認購倍數表示。倍數高，代表申請的人多；但它並不能說明有多少人打算持有、有多少人會在首日早上就沽出，也不能說明認購國際配售部分的機構，之後是增持還是減持。首日的價格，是這些決定在短短幾個小時內互相抵銷的結果，與路演階段所見的需求，未必一樣。

再看新類別為甚麼重要。公司招股書描述的管線包括診斷與治療兩類放射性藥物，其中有處於註冊階段的產品，並說明其中一項核心產品預期在2026年12月於內地獲批。投資者面對一個沒有上市同行可比的新類別，要自行決定用甚麼估值倍數、挑哪些公司作參照；沒有同行，價格發現就更容易受當天氣氛左右。對創辦人而言，實際的結論是：做業界第一，並不自動等於定價上佔優。

無紙化的新制度也值得留意，但單憑一次上市，還看不出它對買賣的影響。在無紙證券制度下，股份紀錄以電子形式保存，經紀、股份過戶登記處與投資者的交收步驟都有所改變。最初幾個星期若出現操作上的摩擦，主要是中介機構要處理的事；不過發行人仍應清楚自己的股東名冊如何運作、聯絡人是誰，以及有股東查詢紀錄時怎麼辦。

一支審慎的18A團隊，可以從中得到三點。第一，要規劃投資者組成，而不只是規劃集資額。向保薦人問清楚預期有多少基石與錨定投資者、鎖定期怎麼安排，以及散戶與機構之間的分配是如何決定的。一份偏重短線散戶需求的訂單簿，在截止認購那天看來很漂亮，一個星期後卻未必那麼穩。

第二，及早準備穩定價格的討論。香港的發售通常設有超額配股安排和穩定價格操作人，其可做的事有明確規則。創辦人應細讀自己招股書的相關章節，弄清楚實際會怎樣運作，而不是假設它能夠承接疲弱的開市。

第三，上市前就寫好第一年的披露日曆，而不是上市後才補。把全年業績、中期業績、臨床數據公布、監管決定和禁售期屆滿的日期逐一列出。上市公司被如何評價，除了消息的內容，也看消息是怎樣、甚麼時候發出的。

上市首日的開市價只是一個觀察點，單憑一個點，對一門生意說不了多少。先通國際之後的股價、註冊階段產品的進度，以及業績，所說的會比首個早上多得多。對考慮來港集資的創辦人，更持久的啟示是程序上的：一份強勁的訂單簿、一份扎實的招股書，以及上市後數週的安排，是三項不同的交付。本文是研究桌面的看法，不構成投資建議，也不是對任何上市公司優劣的評論。
