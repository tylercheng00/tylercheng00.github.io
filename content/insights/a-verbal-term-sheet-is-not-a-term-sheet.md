::: en
People say we are aligned and the room moves as if the round were done. That impulse sharpens this week: APEC business leaders open in Shenzhen, Asia-Pacific investment chatter celebrates China’s rise and the Greater Bay Area, and US screens separately run Jensen Huang, Nvidia, and bipartisan pressure to block more AI chip sales into China. Sentiment for China-linked names can feel briefly warmer in one feed and colder in another. A verbal term sheet is useful in both weathers. It is also where companies start spending like the wire already cleared.

A verbal yes born in summit-week optimism is still interest with a shape. Valuation is only one line. Option pool, liquidation preference, board seats, information rights, China-structure covenants, and what happens if the close slips — those decide whether the verbal yes survives counsel and a partner meeting held after the next chip-control headline. If those points are we can sort it later, you do not have a term sheet. You have a mood. Moods move when leaders leave Shenzhen and when Senate banking coverage refreshes.

Ask for the open points in writing before you change runway assumptions. One email is enough: the number discussed, the open legal points, who must approve, and the date by which a draft term sheet appears or the conversation is marked cold. If the other side will not put that in writing during a week when everyone is “constructive” about Asia investment, believe the refusal more than the warmth. Constructive language is cheap while cameras are on regional cooperation. Paper is the filter.

Keep verbal agreements in the same list as conditional yeses. Owner, date, what document will make it real. Celebrate when signatures land. Until then, the calendar still belongs to diligence, not to the party. Diligence for China-linked founders seeking US capital this week will include the usual financials plus questions that rhyme with the news: where IP sits, which entities touch controlled technology, how you describe China exposure in one page. A verbal valuation that assumed none of that work is unfinished homework, not a close.

Watch spending behaviour. Teams hear “aligned on terms” and accelerate hiring, lab spend, or exclusive banker retainers as if the round were in escrow. If the close slips because export-control politics cooled a committee, you have burned months of runway on a conversation. Match spend to signed paper. Soft circles and verbal sheets do not refill the bank.

Build a two-column tracker. Left: what was said in the room — valuation range, lead appetite, timing. Right: what exists on letterhead — nothing, draft, signed. Update the right column only when files move. Share the tracker with your board so summit-week euphoria does not become an undocumented governance story. Boards that only hear “we are close” during a loud diplomacy week learn the wrong lesson when “close” evaporates.

Capital-markets implication is practical, not prophetic. US investors can be constructive about Greater Bay Area operating stories and still freeze a verbal yes when Nvidia politics reminds the partnership of control risk. You cannot legislate their freeze. You can refuse to treat their warmth as a wire. If they want exclusivity while the term sheet is still verbal, attach a short clock and a document list. Exclusivity without paper is how founders lose parallel options during the one week when every banker claims urgency because APEC is in town.

Founders also use verbal sheets to avoid internal sequencing. Science wants another run; finance wants to announce; counsel wants two weeks on disclosures. A spoken number becomes a substitute for choosing the sequence. Choose the sequence first. Which gate unlocks a written sheet. Which spend buys that gate. Which date you will either receive paper or reopen other conversations. The market can shout about Shenzhen and chips while you do that work.

Keep the voice practical. No investment advice. No invented personal closes. No claim that today’s summit predicts tomorrow’s signatures. Just the rule that survives friendly weeks and hostile ones: until the paper is signed, treat the conversation as interest with a shape, not as a close. When signatures land, celebrate. Until then, protect the calendar and the cash.

有人說我們對齊了，房間氣氛就像這輪已經做完。本週這股衝動更明顯：APEC 工商領袖在深圳開幕，亞太投資議論在慶祝中國崛起與大灣區；美國螢幕另一邊則是黃仁勳、英偉達，以及兩黨推動進一步限制對華人工智能芯片銷售。與中國相關的名字，情緒可以在一個新聞報道短暫變暖、在另一個變冷。口頭投資條款清單在冷暖兩種市況都有用；公司也常在這裏開始像錢已到賬一樣花錢。

峰會週樂觀裏的口頭答應，仍只是有輪廓的興趣。估值只是一行。期權池、清算優先、董事會席位、資訊權、中國結構契諾、交割若延遲怎麼辦——這些才決定口頭答應能不能過律師關，以及下一則芯片管制標題之後的合夥人會議。若這些點都是稍後再弄，你沒有投資條款清單，只有氣氛。領袖離開深圳、參議院銀行報道一更新，氣氛就會變。

在你改跑道假設之前，要求把未決點寫下來。一封電郵就夠：談過的數字、未決法律點、誰必須批准、草稿投資條款清單出現或對話標為冷卻的日期。若對方在人人對亞洲投資都「建設性」的一週仍不肯寫下來，相信拒絕，多過相信溫暖。鏡頭還在區域合作上時，建設性語言很便宜。紙才是過濾器。

把口頭協議和有條件的答應放在同一份清單。負責人、日期、哪份文件會讓它變真。簽名落地再慶祝。在那之前，日曆仍屬於盡職審查，不屬於慶功。本週為尋求美國資本的中國相關創辦人做的盡職，會包括慣常財務，外加與新聞押韻的問題：知識產權在哪、哪些主體碰受控技術、你如何用一頁描述中國暴露。假設那些工作不必做的口頭估值，是未完成的作業，不是交割。

留意支出行為。團隊聽到「條款已對齊」，就加速招聘、實驗室開支或排他銀行顧問聘用，彷彿這輪已在託管。若交割因出口管制政治冷卻了委員會而滑掉，你已把幾個月跑道燒在一次對話上。支出對齊已簽的紙。軟意向與口頭條款清單不會把銀行戶口填滿。

做兩欄追蹤。左：房間裏說了甚麼——估值區間、領投意願、時間。右：信箋上存在甚麼——沒有、草稿、已簽。只有檔案移動時才更新右欄。與董事會分享追蹤表，免得峰會週亢奮變成沒有記錄的管治故事。在嘈雜外交週只聽到「我們很接近」的董事會，會在「接近」蒸發時學到錯誤的一課。

對資本市場的含義是實務的，不是預言。美國投資者可以對大灣區營運故事建設性，卻仍在英偉達政治提醒合夥人管制風險時凍結口頭答應。你立法不了他們的凍結。你可以拒絕把他們的溫暖當成電匯。若他們想在投資條款清單仍口頭時要排他，附上短時鐘與文件清單。沒有紙的排他，是創辦人在每個銀行家都因 APEC 在城而宣稱緊急的一週，丟掉平行選項的方式。

創辦人也用口頭條款逃避內部排序。科學想再跑一輪；財務想宣布；律師想披露再兩星期。口說的數字變成選擇次序的替代品。先選次序。哪個關口打開書面條款。哪筆支出買到那個關口。哪個日期你會收到紙，或重開其他對話。市場可以一邊喊深圳與芯片，你一邊做那份工。

語氣保持實務。沒有投資建議。不編造個人交割。不宣稱今天的峰會能預測明天的簽名。只堅持友善週與敵意週都活得過的規則：紙未簽之前，把對話當成有形狀的興趣，不是交割。簽名落地再慶祝。在那之前，保護日曆與現金。
