Buried under the applause on 23 November 2026 was a sober clause: Nvidia’s independent-director investigation would continue to conclusion, export-control oversight would thicken, and a chief compliance officer search would proceed. Markets can mark a CEO back to hero status in an hour. Diligence cannot mark a file closed because a press release felt conclusive.

Apply the same standard internally. If a partner asked for a China memo, a related-party schedule, or a key-person plan during Huang’s absence, those requests remain open until the artefacts exist — even if the public story flipped. Flips are not deliveries.

Update the open-question log with statuses, not vibes. “In counsel review until date.” “Draft attached.” “Blocked on customer NDA.” Status language keeps trust when headlines whip. Vibe language (“should be fine now that he’s back”) destroys trust.

Teach the team that reinstatement news is context, not completion. Completion is a file in the data room with a hash date. Context is what the homepage of every newspaper shows. You need both. You fundraise on completion.

Close the day by sending the open-question log to the lead partner without commentary on politics. Let the log prove you understand that investigations — theirs and yours — end with documents, not with cheers.

Keep the day hook in the first paragraph and the operating proof in the rest. Investors forgive topical openings; they do not forgive empty middles. Add dates, owners, and artefacts that can survive a partner forward. If a control does not yet exist, name the date it will. If a risk cannot be closed this week, name the mitigation already funded in cash runway weeks. Write as if the next associate will paste your note into an investment committee chat without you in the room.
