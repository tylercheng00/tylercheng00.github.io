The first questions I usually hear about a US listing are about money. What valuation can we get? What will the underwriters charge? How much will the lawyers cost?

They are fair questions. They are just not the ones that decide whether the listing happens.

What decides it is the calendar.

### Everything runs in parallel, and everything depends on everything else

A US IPO is not one project. It is a dozen projects that have to finish in the same week. The audit has to be done by a PCAOB-registered firm, usually covering two or more years. The group may need restructuring into a listing vehicle. If there are operations in mainland China, the CSRC filing has to be planned in. The registration statement goes to the SEC, and the comments come back in rounds. Then the exchange, the roadshow, the pricing.

None of these is especially mysterious. The difficulty is that one slipping workstream moves all the others. A late audit adjustment means updated numbers in the filing, which means another round of comments, which can push you past the window you were aiming for.

### Windows close

Markets open and shut. The listing that is ready when investors are buying beats the perfect listing that arrives three months later. I would rather see a company go with a clean, slightly conservative story on time than polish a story that misses its moment.

### What I would do differently, if starting again

- **Start the audit and governance work 12 to 18 months before you think you need it.** It is almost always the long pole.
- **Give one person the master timetable.** Not a committee. One owner, one document, updated weekly.
- **Keep a decisions log.** Half the delays I have seen came from re-opening questions that had already been answered.
- **Ask honestly why the US.** If your closest comparable companies trade in Hong Kong, that may be the better home. A US listing is a tool, not a trophy.

Money matters. But in my experience the companies that list well are the ones that respect the calendar first.

談到赴美上市，我最常聽到的第一批問題都和錢有關：可以拿到甚麼估值？承銷商收多少？律師費要多少？

這些問題很合理，只是它們並不決定上市能否成事。

決定成敗的，是時間表。

### 所有事情同時進行，而且互相牽連

美股 IPO 不是一個項目，而是十多個必須在同一星期完成的項目。審計要由 PCAOB 註冊的會計師事務所完成，通常涵蓋兩年或以上。集團可能要重組成上市主體。業務在中國內地的，要把中國證監會的備案計算在內。註冊聲明遞交 SEC 之後，意見會一輪一輪地回來。之後還有交易所、路演和定價。

每一項本身都不算神秘。難處在於：任何一條工作線延誤，都會拖動其他所有工作線。審計遲來一個調整，招股文件的數字就要更新，就可能多一輪 SEC 意見，然後便錯過原本瞄準的時間窗口。

### 窗口會關上

市場會開，也會關。在投資者願意買的時候準備好上市，勝過三個月後才出現的「完美」上市。我寧願看到一家公司帶著清晰、略為保守的故事準時出發，也不想看到它把故事打磨得完美卻錯過時機。

### 如果重新開始，我會這樣做

- **比你以為需要的時間，提早 12 至 18 個月開始審計及管治工作。** 這幾乎永遠是最長的那條工作線。
- **由一個人負責總時間表。** 不是委員會，而是一個負責人、一份文件、每星期更新。
- **保留決策紀錄。** 我見過的延誤，有一半來自重新討論早已決定的問題。
- **誠實地問：為甚麼是美國？** 如果最接近你的同業都在香港上市，香港可能更適合你。赴美上市是工具，不是獎盃。

錢當然重要。但在我看來，上市做得好的公司，都是先尊重時間表的公司。
