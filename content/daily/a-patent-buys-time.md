::: en
A patent slide often arrives early, with a number and a map. The feeling it is meant to create is: we own this. On the day of the raise, a filing is usually not revenue. It is a place in line.

Three facts get collapsed into "we have IP". An application is a request. A granted patent is a right, in one country, with claims that survived an examiner, often narrower than what you wanted. Ownership is a chain of assignments from every person who contributed, including the student and the contractor who was "just helping". If a link is missing, the right sits in the wrong place, and a count of filings does not repair it.

The slide I trust has those on separate lines. How many applications. How many grants, and where. One sentence that every inventor has assigned to the company that is raising. If the third sentence is not true, fix that before you file anything new.

Investors who are not patent lawyers will not read the document. You still need to say, in ordinary words, what a competitor is not allowed to do if the claims you care about are granted. If you can only point at a diagram and hope, the claims are a description, not a fence. Ask counsel for that sentence in writing. "We hope to cover the method of making it" is a fine sentence. "Protected worldwide" is not, unless you can point at the grants.

Filing in many countries feels like ambition. Each one is a future invoice: examination, translation, annuities, local counsel. A young company can file itself into a fixed cost it cannot carry, then drop families just as they get interesting. Choose countries the way you choose a first market. Where a competitor would actually make or sell. Where you can pay for the next five years without pretending the next round is guaranteed.

A filing date holds your place against later filers. That is real, when the idea is ready to be described. It does not stop a well-funded team working around you, and it does not make a customer buy. Use the time to get the assignments clean and the product real enough that the patent is attached to something a buyer can recognise. A fence around an empty field is still a fence. It is not yet a business.

專利那頁常常來得很早，帶着一個數字和一張地圖。它想製造的感覺是：這是我們的。融資的這一天，一份申請通常不是收入。它是排隊的位置。

三件不同的事被壓進「我們有知識產權」。申請是一個請求。授權是一項權利，在一個國家，權利要求活過了審查員，常常比你想要的窄。擁有權是一條從每個貢獻者轉讓過來的鏈，包括那個學生、和那位「只是幫忙」的承包商。任何一環缺了，權利就坐在錯的地方，申請的數目補不了。

我信的那一頁把這三件分開。多少份申請。多少項授權，在哪。一句：每位發明人都已經轉讓給這家正在融資的公司。第三句不是真的，先修這個，再交新的。

不是專利律師的投資者不會讀那份文件。你仍然要用普通的話講：你在乎的那些權利要求如果被授權，競爭對手不可以做甚麼。你只能指着圖然後希望對方明白，權利要求就還是一段描述，不是圍欄。請律師把那句寫下來。「我們希望覆蓋製造方法」是一句可以的話。「全球受保護」不是，除非你指得出那些授權。

很多國家一起申請，感覺像野心。每一個都是將來的發票：審查、翻譯、年費、當地律師。年輕公司可以把自己申請到一個付不起的固定成本，然後在專利族變得有趣的時候放棄。選國家像選第一個市場。競爭對手真的會生產或賣的地方。未來五年你付得起、而不必假裝下一輪已保證的地方。

申請日讓你在後來的人面前佔着位置。想法已經準備好被描述的時候，這是真的，值得付。它阻止不了一個有錢的團隊繞過你，也不會讓客戶買。用這段時間把轉讓弄乾淨，把產品做得實在到專利是附着在買家認得出的東西上。空地周圍的圍欄仍然是圍欄。它還不是一門生意。
