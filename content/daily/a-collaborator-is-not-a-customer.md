::: en
A slide says "in discussion with" three hospitals and a global company. Another slide says a professor at a well-known institute is a collaborator. The room hears demand. Often what exists is a polite scientific interest, a material transfer that has not been signed, and a logo that was used too early.

Interest is good. It is not traction. A collaborator is not a customer, and calling them one makes the real customers harder to recognise later.

### What was signed, and what was merely said

I want a three-column list. Who. What they have actually signed. What they have actually done. A signed material transfer, a paid pilot, a joint grant with a budget, a letter that commits to a study and names a date: those are events. "They are very keen" is a mood. Moods belong in the speaker's voice, not in the pipeline slide.

If the honest list is short, let it be short. One paid pilot is a stronger slide than five unnamed discussions, because a reader can finish the sentence.

### Logos have a cost

Using an institution's name before their contracts office would recognise the relationship creates a specific kind of trouble. The trouble arrives later, when a real agreement is close and someone senior sees their mark on a deck they did not approve. Scientific enthusiasm and institutional permission are different people. Do not spend the second in order to decorate the first.

Ask, in writing, before the logo goes on the page. If the answer is slow, use a description instead: "a public hospital in southern China", "a top-ten pharmaceutical company by revenue". You lose a little glamour. You keep the relationship.

### An academic collaborator is paid in a different currency

A professor may want a paper, a student funded, a reagent they cannot easily buy. That can be a fair exchange, and it can produce real data. It is still not revenue, and it is not validation that a buyer with a budget will pay. When you report it, report it as science. The moment you put it in the commercial section, a careful investor starts checking every other line in that section too.

### The question that sorts them

Ask each partner, gently, what they would lose if you disappeared in six months. A customer loses a supply, a data stream, a slot in a study they have scheduled. A curious collaborator loses an interesting meeting. Both people are worth having. Only one of them belongs in the sentence that begins "the market is pulling".

Keep the collaborators. Just do not invoice them in the story before they have paid, in money or in a commitment you could show a stranger.

::: zh
一頁投影片寫着「正與」三家醫院和一家跨國公司「洽談」。另一頁寫一位著名研究院的教授是合作方。房間聽到的是需求。實際存在的，常常是一份禮貌的科學興趣、一份還沒簽的材料轉移，和一個用得太早的標誌。

興趣是好的。它不是進展。合作方不是客戶。把他們叫成客戶，會讓你以後更難認出真正的客戶。

### 簽了甚麼，只是說了甚麼

我要一張三欄的表。誰。他們實際簽了甚麼。他們實際做了甚麼。簽了的材料轉移、付了錢的試驗、有預算的聯合課題、一封承諾研究並寫明日期的信：這些是事件。「他們很積極」是一種情緒。情緒屬於說話的語氣，不屬於管道那一頁。

如果誠實的清單很短，就讓它短。一個付了錢的試驗，好過五個沒有名字的洽談，因為讀者可以把句子讀完。

### 標誌是有代價的

在對方的合約部門還不承認這段關係之前就用機構的名字，會製造一種特定的麻煩。麻煩來得較晚：真正的協議快要成的時候，一位高層看到自己的標誌出現在一份他們沒批准的簡報上。科學上的熱忱，和機構的許可，是不同的人。不要為了裝飾前者而花掉後者。

標誌上頁之前，用書面問一句。如果回覆很慢，就改用描述：「華南一家公立醫院」，「以收入計的前十名藥企」。你少一點光彩。你保住關係。

### 學術合作方收的是另一種貨幣

一位教授也許想要一篇論文、一個有資助的學生、一種他們不容易買到的試劑。這可以是公平的交換，也可以產生真數據。它仍然不是收入，也不是證明一個有預算的買家會付錢。報告它的時候，把它報告成科學。一旦你把它放進商業那一節，謹慎的投資者會開始檢查那一節的每一行。

### 把他們分開的那個問題

溫和地問每一位夥伴：如果六個月後你消失，他們會失去甚麼。客戶失去的是供應、一條數據、一個已經排期的研究位置。好奇的合作方失去的是一次有趣的會議。兩種人都值得有。只有一種人屬於那句「市場在拉我們」開頭的話。

留着合作方。只是在他們付錢之前——用錢，或用一份你能給陌生人看的承諾——不要在故事裏向他們開出發票。
