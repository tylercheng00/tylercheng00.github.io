Hong Kong woke up the day after APEC Shenzhen with a familiar capital-markets hangover: leaders had met, Wang Yi briefed outcomes in Shenzhen, and inboxes filled with “while everyone is still in town” introductions. Summit weeks compress social graphs. People who would normally take three weeks to connect two founders suddenly offer a name, a WeChat, and a half-promise. That generosity is real. It is also fragile. The introduction that survives the next forty-eight hours is the one the helper can forward without rewriting who you are.

A forwardable blurb is not a biography. It is four to six lines that protect the helper’s reputation. Lead with who you help and in what market. Add one sentence of proof a stranger can verify — a signed customer, a cleared regulatory step, a closed prior round with a named lead, not “strong traction.” Put one link, not five. End with a single ask: a thirty-minute call, a warm intro to a named function, a review of a specific diligence page. If the helper has to invent any of those lines, the message waits until Monday. Monday never comes after a summit week; another thread does.

Write the blurb in Chinese and in English before you ask for the favour. Post-APEC rooms in Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Singapore move bilingual. A partner who forwards only your English paragraph into a Mandarin chain forces the next person to translate under time pressure. That translation becomes a paraphrase. The paraphrase becomes a misunderstanding. The misunderstanding becomes a polite “let’s reconnect after the holidays.” You did not lose the introduction in the room. You lost it in the forward.

Choose one story. Summit weeks tempt founders to stack every theme that just dominated the feed — AI chips, telecom scrutiny, China growth, US listing readiness — into one paragraph so the blurb feels “current.” Current is not the same as clear. Pick the lane that matches the person being introduced. If the warm path is a US growth fund, lead with unit economics and US process readiness. If it is a Greater China strategic, lead with operating map and customer proof. The helper should not have to decide which version of you to send. That decision is yours, and it belongs in the draft you paste to them.

Respect the helper’s name. People offer introductions after summit weeks because they want to be useful while the calendar is hot. They do not want to be the person who forwarded a foggy deck link and a five-bullet autobiography. Give them language that sounds like them: short, concrete, no hype adjectives. If your only proof is still qualitative, say so in one honest clause and ask for advice rather than a hard intro. Honesty travels. Fog does not.

Test the blurb the way you test a risk factor. Hand it to a colleague who was not in your last meeting. Ask them to forward it to an imaginary partner without editing. If they hesitate, the blurb is not ready. If they add a sentence of explanation, cut that sentence into the draft and delete the rest. The goal is paste-ready text, not a prompt for improvisation.

Timing matters after a leaders meeting. The first wave arrives while people are still in the same city; the second arrives when they are back at desks sorting which favours were real. Have the blurb ready for both. A clean six-line note plus one current one-pager beats a twenty-slide file that arrives after the helper has spent their social capital elsewhere.

What not to do matters as much as what to write. Do not attach three deck versions “depending on interest.” Do not list every advisor. Do not open with APEC, Trump–Xi, or chip-policy commentary unless the recipient’s job is policy. The summit is why the introduction arrived. It is not the content of the introduction.

If you cannot write the blurb in ten minutes, you are not ready for the introduction you want. Finish who you help, the one proof, and the one ask. Thank the helper in a separate line that does not dilute the paste block. Summit weeks create noise. A forwardable blurb is how a serious company cuts through it without asking friends to edit for free.

深圳亞太經合組織（APEC）領袖會議剛落幕，王毅在深圳通報會議成果的同一時段，香港、深圳與新加坡的信箱往往同步湧進「人還在城裏、順手牽線」的引薦。峰會週會壓縮人際網絡：平常要三週才肯介紹的兩個人，這時可能突然丟出一個名字、一個微信、半句承諾。好意是真的，卻也很脆。能熬過接下來四十八小時的，通常不是最華麗的簡報，而是對方不用重寫、直接轉得出去的那段自我介紹。

所謂「轉發得了」，不是自傳，而是四到六行、能護住介紹人面子的話。先寫你服務誰、在哪個市場；再給一句外人核得了的證明——已簽約客戶、已過的監管節點、已關閉且具名的上一輪，而不是「動能強勁」。只放一個連結。最後只提一個請求：三十分鐘電話、引薦到某個明確職能、或請對方看某一頁盡調。介紹人若還要替你發明其中任何一句，訊息就會進草稿匣；峰會後的星期一從來不會空下來，只會被下一條線擠掉。

開口求人情之前，先備好中文與英文兩個版本。峰會後的大中華與跨岸房間本來就是雙語作業。若你只丟英文段落進中文群，下一棒就得在趕時間時翻譯；翻譯變成改寫，改寫變成誤解，誤解再變成客氣的「假期後再連」。引薦不是死在會議室，是死在轉發鏈。

還要強迫自己只講一個故事。峰會週最容易把新聞熱點——人工智能芯片、電信審查、中國增長、赴美上市準備——全塞進同一段，好讓自己顯得「跟得上」。跟得上不等於清楚。依被介紹對象選賽道：若路徑是美國成長基金，就領先單位經濟與美國流程就緒；若是大中華策略方，就領先營運版圖與客戶證據。介紹人不該替你決定寄哪一個版本；那個決定必須寫在你貼給他的草稿裏。

也要尊重介紹人的名字。峰會後願意牽線，多半是想在日程還熱時幫一點忙，而不是成為那個轉發了含糊簡報連結與五點自傳的人。給他們聽起來像他們會說的話：短、具體、少形容詞。若證明仍偏定性，就用一句老實話說明，並改求建議而非硬性引薦。老實話傳得動，含糊話傳不動。

測試方法不妨像測風險因素：把這段話交給沒參加你上次會議的同事，請他不改一字轉給想像中的合夥人。若他猶豫，代表還沒好；若他忍不住加一句解釋，就把那句收進正文，其餘刪掉。目標是可直接貼上的文字，不是即興創作的提示詞。

時間點同樣關鍵。第一波在人還同城時；第二波在各自回辦公室、分辨哪些人情是真的之後。兩個波次都該有現成文案。乾淨的六行字加一頁現況說明，往往勝過介紹人社交資本已花在別處之後才抵達的二十頁精美檔。

不該做的事與該寫的事一樣重要。不要附「視興趣而定」的三個版本；不要攤開全部顧問；除非對方本職是政策，否則不要用APEC、特朗普與習近平、或芯片管制當開場。峰會是引薦出現的原因，不是引薦的內容。

若十分鐘內寫不出這段話，多半還沒準備好你想要的那次引薦。先把「你幫誰」、那一句證明、那一個請求寫完，再另起一行謝謝介紹人，別把感謝混進可轉發區塊。峰會週製造噪音；一段轉發得了的中英介紹，是認真公司在不麻煩朋友當義務編輯的前提下穿過噪音的方式。
