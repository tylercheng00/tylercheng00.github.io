::: en
"Send me the deck" feels like progress. Someone wants to read. Often it is the opposite. It is a courteous way to leave a conversation that has not earned a second meeting, and to do it without saying no in the room.

I do not treat that sentence as a pipeline. I treat it as a question I failed to answer yet.

### Ask what they would need to meet again

Before you attach the file, ask one thing: if the deck is what you hope, what happens next, and what would have to be true? A serious person will tell you. They want the unit economics, or the IP opinion, or a customer who will take a call. An unserious person will say "I'll have a look and revert." Both answers are useful. Only one of them is a next step.

Then send exactly that, plus the deck if they still want it. A folder aimed at the stated objection beats a generic sixteen-page story sent into silence.

### Do not refresh the relationship with "just following up"

A week later, "just checking you received it" adds no information. It asks them to do work you already asked them to do. If you have nothing new, wait. If you have something new — a number, a signed contract, a date — send that, in four lines, and do not apologise for existing.

The founders who get answers are usually the ones who make the reply easy. A question with a yes or a no. A time. A specific page. Not a nudge.

### The deck still has to stand alone

Sometimes the request is genuine, and the reader is on a plane, or in a Monday meeting where you are item seven. Write the first two pages for that reader. What you do. For whom. Why it is possible now. What this round buys. What you want from them. If those pages only make sense when you are presenting, they are speaker notes, not a deck, and they will not survive the inbox.

### Keep a record of what was actually asked

Not a CRM sermon. A line: who, date, what they asked for, what you sent, what they said would come next. Six weeks later you will not remember, and you will either pester the wrong person or forget the one who asked a real question. The list also stops you fooling yourself about how many "live" conversations you have. A conversation is live when the other side owes you a specific response. Everything else is a wish with a logo on it.

Send the deck when it serves a question. Do not send it as a substitute for one.

::: zh
「把簡報發來」感覺像進展。有人想讀。常常剛好相反。這是一種有禮的離開：這次對話還沒有贏得第二次會議，而又不必在房間裏說不。

我不把這句話當成管道。我把它當成一個我還沒有答上的問題。

### 問他們怎樣才會再見

在你附上檔案之前，先問一件事：如果簡報如你所願，下一步是甚麼，而甚麼必須是真的？認真的人會告訴你。他們要單位經濟、知識產權意見，或者一個願意接電話的客戶。不認真的人會說「我看看再回」。兩個答案都有用。只有一個是下一步。

然後就送那一件，如果他們仍要簡報，再附上。一個對準對方說出來的疑慮的資料夾，好過一份掉進沉默的十六頁通用故事。

### 不要用「跟進一下」來刷新關係

一星期後，「確認你收到沒有」沒有增加任何資訊。它是請對方做你已經請他們做的工作。如果你沒有新的東西，就等。如果你有——一個數字、一份簽了的合約、一個日期——用四行送出，不要為自己的存在道歉。

拿得到回覆的創辦人，通常是讓回覆變得容易的人。一個可以答是或否的問題。一個時間。某一頁。不是一下輕推。

### 簡報仍然要自己站得住

有時這個要求是真的。讀者在飛機上，或者在星期一的會議裏，你是第七項。為那個讀者寫頭兩頁。你做甚麼。為誰。為甚麼現在可能。這一輪買到甚麼。你想他們做甚麼。如果這兩頁只有你在講的時候才說得通，它們是講者備註，不是簡報，它們過不了收件箱。

### 記下對方真正問了甚麼

不是一套客戶管理系統的說教。一行：誰、日期、他們要甚麼、你送了甚麼、他們說下一步是甚麼。六星期後你不會記得，然後你要麼去煩錯的人，要麼忘了那個問了真問題的人。這張表也阻止你騙自己：你有多少個「活着」的對話。只有對方欠你一個具體回覆時，對話才是活的。其餘的，是一個貼了標誌的願望。

當簡報服務於一個問題，才送出。不要用它代替問題。
