::: en
Founders sometimes skip board notes because nothing big happened inside the company. Outside, plenty is happening: APEC business leaders open in Shenzhen, Asia-Pacific trade and investment week puts China’s rise and the Greater Bay Area in the foreground, and US coverage of Jensen Huang, Nvidia, and AI chip sales into China keeps control politics loud. That external noise is exactly when the board most needs a line from you — not a geopolitics essay, a company line: what moved, what did not, what you need from them.

Keep it boring on purpose. Cash runway in weeks. One hiring or contracting decision. One diligence item that is stuck. One ask. If you only write when there is a win, the board learns that silence means trouble. In a week when markets reprice China-linked risk off diplomacy and chip headlines, silence also means the board will fill the gap with whatever they read on their phones. Your short note is how you replace rumour with your operating facts.

A weekly or biweekly note also protects you. When a later debate starts about what we knew when — about a slipped close, a customer pause, a counsel flag on China exposure — you have a dated trail. Theatre is a polished deck with no numbers timed for summit photos. A plain email with three facts is work. US investors and directors watching China-touching companies this week will mix fair governance questions with mood. Answer governance with cadence. Park mood with one sentence and a return to runway, gates, and asks.

Write the note so it survives a partner who just read export-control coverage. Do not open with “given APEC optimism” or “given Nvidia news.” Open with cash, milestones, and decisions. Then, if relevant, one clause on how external noise touches your calendar: a diligence question that arrived, a meeting that slipped, a disclosure you are drafting. That clause shows you are awake without turning the board pack into cable news. Boards fund companies. They do not need you to narrate summit choreography.

Structure helps under distraction. Use the same four headings every time: Cash, Product or science, Financing process, Ask. Under Financing process, name whether anything is verbal, drafted, or signed — especially this week, when summit warmth produces soft circles that feel like progress. Under Ask, make it specific: an introduction, a decision on a bridge endgame, a review of the China-operating one-pager before it goes to a US reader. Vague asks invite vague silence.

Capital implications for China-linked founders seeking US money are about trust mechanics. Funds that will own your stock through listing or late-stage rounds notice who communicates when the tape is noisy. A founder who disappears during APEC week and reappears with a crisis after chip politics moved a term sheet looks unmanaged. A founder who sent two dull notes with dates looks like someone who can run a public-company calendar later. Dull is a feature.

Do not wait for a “board-worthy” event. The absence of movement is information: assay still transferring, customer still unpaid, counsel still on the exposure page. Reporting no movement on a dated line is cleaner than a surprise three weeks later. If something did move because of the news cycle — a strategic asked about supply-chain politics, a banker requested a China map — say so in one factual bullet. Attribution beats vibes.

Protect the difference between update and performance. Performance is a long narrative about regional destiny and AI-era opportunity. Update is numbers, owners, and dates. In a week already saturated with regional destiny talk from Shenzhen stages, your board does not need more atmosphere from you. They need to know whether you can still make payroll, hit the next gate, and answer a US diligence list without improvising.

If you have been silent, send a catch-up note today with the last four weeks in bullets and the next two decisions you need. Same-day honesty repairs more than a perfect retrospective. Investors dislike surprises more than hard news. A short, dated update is how you keep hard news from becoming a surprise while Shenzhen hosts leaders and Washington argues about chips. Outside noise continues either way. Inside, the controllable variable is whether your board hears from you before they hear from the feed.

創辦人有時因為公司裏面沒有大事，就跳過董事會更新。外面卻很熱鬧：APEC 工商領袖在深圳開幕，亞太貿易與投資週把中國崛起與大灣區推上版面，美國對黃仁勳、英偉達與對華人工智能芯片銷售的報道又把管制政治炒得很大聲。外面愈嘈，董事會愈需要你一行字——不是地緣政治長文，而是公司現況：甚麼動了、甚麼沒動、你需要他們做甚麼。

刻意寫得無聊。現金跑道還有幾週。一個人事或外包決定。一項卡住的盡職審查。一個請求。若只在有勝仗時才寫，董事會會學會：沉默等於出事。市場按外交與芯片標題替中國相關風險重新定價的一週，沉默也意味着董事會會用手機上讀到的東西填補空白。你的短訊，是用營運事實替換傳聞的方法。

每週或隔週的短訊也保護你。後來爭論當時知道甚麼時——交割滑了、客戶停了、律師對中國暴露打了旗——你有帶日期的痕跡。演戲是配合峰會合照、沒有數字的精美簡報。三件事實的普通電郵才是工作。本週看着碰觸中國公司的美國投資者與董事，會把公平的管治問題與情緒混在一起。用節奏回答管治。用一句話停住情緒，回到跑道、關口與請求。

把短訊寫成能活過剛讀完出口管制報道的合夥人。不要用「鑒於 APEC 樂觀」或「鑒於英偉達新聞」開頭。用現金、里程碑與決定開頭。然後，若相關，用一句寫外部噪音如何碰到你的日曆：來了一個盡職問題、一個會議滑了、你正在起草一項披露。那一句表明你醒着，卻不把董事會材料變成有線新聞。董事會資助公司。他們不需要你旁述峰會編排。

分心時，結構有幫助。每次用同一四個標題：現金、產品或科學、融資流程、請求。在融資流程下，寫明甚麼是口頭、草稿或已簽——尤其本週，峰會溫暖會製造感覺像進度的軟意向。在請求下，寫具體：一個介紹、橋接收尾的決定、中國營運一頁紙送給美國讀者前的審閱。含糊請求引來含糊沉默。

對尋求美國資金的中國相關創辦人，資本含義關乎信任機制。會透過上市或後期輪次持有你股票的基金，會注意行情嘈雜時誰在溝通。在 APEC 週消失、芯片政治推動條款後才帶危機出現的創辦人，看起來像失控。寄過兩則帶日期的無聊短訊的創辦人，看起來像以後能跑上市公司日曆的人。無聊是優點。

不要等「值得上董事會」的事件。沒有移動本身就是信息：檢測仍在轉移、客戶仍未付費、律師仍在暴露頁上。在帶日期的一行報告沒有移動，比三週後的驚喜乾淨。若因新聞周期真的動了——戰略方問供應鏈政治、銀行家要中國地圖——用一條事實子彈寫出來。歸因勝過氣氛。

分清更新與演戲。演戲是關於區域命運與人工智能時代機會的長敘事。更新是數字、負責人與日期。在深圳舞台已經充滿區域命運談話的一週，你的董事會不需要你再添氣氛。他們需要知道你是否仍能發薪、打中下一關、回答美國盡職清單而不即興。

若你一直沉默，今天就發一則補課短訊：過去四週用子彈列出，加上你需要的下兩個決定。當天誠實，比完美回顧更能修復。投資者不喜歡驚喜，甚於不喜歡硬消息。一則短而有日期的更新，是在深圳接待領袖、華盛頓爭論芯片時，讓硬消息不變驚喜的方法。外面的噪音無論如何會繼續。裏面可控的變量，是董事會在聽新聞報道之前，有沒有先聽到你。
