On Wednesday, 25 November 2026, Bristol Myers Squibb announced topline results from two pivotal Phase 3 trials of Sotyktu (deucravacitinib), an oral TYK2 inhibitor, in adults with moderate-to-severe systemic lupus erythematosus. The two trials were designed alike. POETYK SLE-1 enrolled 510 adults and POETYK SLE-2 enrolled 513. Each compared Sotyktu 3 mg twice daily with placebo on top of background standard of care, and each used the same primary endpoint: the proportion of patients reaching an SLE Responder Index-4, or SRI-4, response at week 52. In SLE-1 the primary endpoint was met, and so were secondary endpoints for a second composite measure called BICLA, for SRI-4 response with a steroid taper, and for patient-reported fatigue. In SLE-2 the primary endpoint was not met. The company said Sotyktu’s response level was consistent with SLE-1, but the placebo group responded more than it did in SLE-1. Numerical improvements favoured Sotyktu across all key secondary endpoints in SLE-2. Safety was described as consistent with the drug’s known profile, and discontinuations because of adverse events were low. The release came on the Wednesday before the U.S. Thanksgiving market holiday, so the reaction played out in a thin, shortened trading week.

Two things were not disclosed. The company did not release response rates, effect sizes or p-values for either trial, and trade press noted this. And it has not announced a regulatory submission for lupus. BMS said it will evaluate data from its Phase 2 and Phase 3 programmes together, talk to health authorities and present detailed results at a future medical meeting. Sotyktu is already approved for psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis, so this readout is about expansion, not about the drug’s existence.

The most useful way to read a split like this is to resist a simple verdict. A result that says “one succeeded and one failed” invites the headline “mixed”. But the company’s own explanation points somewhere more specific: the active arm looked similar across the two trials, while the control arm did not. That is a statement about the comparator, not necessarily about the drug, although until the full data are public nobody outside the company can check it.

Why does placebo response matter so much in lupus? The disease is heterogeneous. Patients differ in organ involvement, flare patterns and background medicines, and composite scores such as SRI-4 add together several clinical measures, so a small shift in how one component moves in the control arm can change the outcome. Placebo response is a recognised challenge in lupus trials generally. Steroid use adds another variable, which is why a secondary endpoint that tracks steroid tapering is more than a footnote.

For a private company thinking about its own immunology programme, there are four practical points.

First, choose and justify the primary endpoint early. SRI-4 and BICLA are both accepted ways to measure lupus activity, and they can behave differently. Know which one your regulators and your investors expect, and why.

Second, write the background-therapy rules before the first patient is enrolled. Steroid tapering, permitted immunosuppressants and rescue medicine all shape the placebo arm. If those rules are loose, the control group can drift.

Third, plan how you will explain a split result. Two trials with identical designs are an advantage only if the protocol and statistical plan state in advance what you will conclude if they diverge. Pre-specification is far more persuasive than an explanation written after the data arrive.

Fourth, decide what you will disclose and when. A topline announcement without numbers leaves the market, partners and clinicians to infer a great deal. That may be a deliberate choice to hold data for a medical meeting, but it has a cost in credibility. If you choose it, say clearly when the numbers will come.

This note is not a view on Sotyktu’s prospects in lupus, on Bristol Myers Squibb shares or on any competing programme. It is a reminder that in a randomised trial there are always two arms, and the second one is where unexpected results often start. It is a research-desk view of trial design and disclosure, not investment advice.

11月25日（星期三），百時美施貴寶公布口服TYK2抑制劑Sotyktu（deucravacitinib）在中重度系統性紅斑狼瘡成人患者中的兩項關鍵三期試驗頂線結果。兩項試驗的設計相近：POETYK SLE-1納入510人，POETYK SLE-2納入513人，都是在標準治療的基礎上，比較每日兩次服用3毫克Sotyktu與安慰劑，主要終點同樣是第52週達到SRI-4應答的患者比例。SLE-1達到主要終點，同時在另一個綜合指標BICLA、配合減少類固醇的SRI-4應答，以及患者自述疲勞程度三項次要終點上，都有統計學意義。SLE-2則沒有達到主要終點。公司說，Sotyktu組的應答水平與SLE-1一致，只是這次安慰劑組的應答比SLE-1更高；SLE-2所有關鍵次要終點在數值上都偏向Sotyktu。安全性被形容為與已知特徵一致，因不良事件而退出的比例偏低。消息在美國感恩節假期前一天公布，市場的反應是在交投偏淡、又提早收市的一週裏完成的。

有兩件事公司沒有披露。第一，兩項試驗的應答率、效應大小和p值，公司都沒有公布，專業媒體也特別指出了這一點；第二，公司尚未宣布向監管機構提交紅斑狼瘡適應症的申請。百時美施貴寶表示，會綜合評估二期與三期的數據，與衞生當局溝通，並在日後的醫學會議上公布詳細結果。Sotyktu在銀屑病和銀屑病關節炎方面已經獲批，所以這次讀數談的是擴大適應症，而不是這隻藥有沒有存在價值。

怎樣讀這種一勝一負的結果？最好先別急着下結論。「一項成功、一項失敗」很容易被概括成「喜憂參半」，但公司自己的解釋指向一個更具體的地方：兩項試驗中，用藥組的表現看來相近，對照組卻不一樣。這說的是比較對象的問題，不一定是藥物本身的問題；不過在完整數據公開之前，公司以外的人無法核實這個說法。

為甚麼安慰劑組的反應在紅斑狼瘡試驗中特別重要？因為這個病的個體差異很大：患者累及的器官、發作的規律、同時使用的藥物各不相同，而SRI-4這類綜合評分，是把幾項臨床指標加在一起計算的，對照組中任何一項指標稍有變化，都可能改變最後的結果。紅斑狼瘡試驗裏安慰劑反應偏高，本來就是業界公認的難題。類固醇的使用又多了一個變數，所以追蹤類固醇減量的次要終點，不只是附註。

對正在籌劃自家免疫類管線的私人公司來說，有四點實際的提醒。

第一，主要終點要及早選定，並說清楚理由。SRI-4和BICLA都是被接受的紅斑狼瘡活動度量表，但兩者的表現可以很不一樣。你要清楚監管機構與投資者期待哪一個，為甚麼。

第二，在第一位病人入組之前，就寫好背景治療的規則。類固醇如何減量、可以同時用哪些免疫抑制劑、怎樣處理急救用藥，都會塑造安慰劑組的面貌。規則訂得鬆，對照組就可能慢慢漂移。

第三，預先計劃好怎樣解釋分歧的結果。兩項設計相同的試驗，只有在方案和統計計劃事先寫明「如果結果相反，我們會如何判斷」的情況下，才算是優勢。事先訂明的說法，遠比數據出來後才補寫的解釋有說服力。

第四，決定披露甚麼、甚麼時候披露。只公布頂線結論、不給數字，會讓市場、合作夥伴和臨床醫生自行推斷很多東西。這可能是為了把數據留給醫學會議的刻意安排，但在可信度上要付出代價。如果決定這樣做，就要清楚說明數字甚麼時候公布。

本文不是對Sotyktu在紅斑狼瘡的前景、百時美施貴寶股價或任何競爭管線的判斷，只是提醒大家：隨機對照試驗總有兩個組別，而意料之外的結果，往往是從第二個組別開始的。這是研究桌面對試驗設計與披露方式的看法，不構成投資建議。
